    Core temperature rises

    Heat gain outstrips heat loss until proteins begin to denature.

    What is actually happening

    Heat production or environmental heat load exceeds the body's ability to shed it. Vasodilation and sweating compensate until fluid runs out, humidity prevents evaporation, or the regulatory system itself fails. Above roughly 40 degrees, cellular proteins begin to denature and tissue dies.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows environmental heat with exertion, high humidity, dehydration, drugs that impair sweating or raise heat production, stimulant toxicity, thyroid storm, and prolonged seizure activity.

    What you see

    • Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache, cramps, with a preserved mental state
    • Heat stroke: core temperature above 40 with altered mental state, which is the defining feature
    • Hot skin that may be dry in classic heat stroke or still sweaty in exertional heat stroke
    • Confusion, agitation, seizures, then unresponsiveness
    • Tachycardia and eventually cardiovascular collapse
    The trap

    The distinction between heat exhaustion and heat stroke is neurological, not thermal. Any altered mental state in a hot patient means heat stroke and is a genuine emergency where cooling is the treatment and delay causes permanent brain injury. The teaching that heat stroke skin is dry is unreliable: exertional heat stroke patients are often drenched in sweat.
