    Neurons fire uncontrollably

    Excitation overwhelms inhibition and electrical activity spreads without brakes.

    What is actually happening

    Normal brain function depends on a balance between excitatory and inhibitory signalling. When excitation dominates, a group of neurons discharges synchronously and recruits neighbours, and the discharge spreads. Firing neurons consume oxygen and glucose at an enormous rate while contracting muscles produce heat and lactate.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows epilepsy, structural brain lesions, low sodium, low glucose, low oxygen, fever in small children, alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal, stimulant toxicity, eclampsia, and head injury.

    What you see

    • Loss of consciousness with generalised stiffening then rhythmic jerking, in a generalised seizure
    • Focal twitching, altered awareness, or strange sensory experiences in a focal seizure
    • Incontinence and tongue biting, typically the side of the tongue
    • Rising temperature and lactic acidosis if it continues
    • A postictal period of confusion and drowsiness afterward
    The trap

    The seizure itself is usually not what harms the patient. The airway is: they cannot protect it, they may vomit, and they are not ventilating properly while it continues. And a seizure that does not stop, or that recurs without recovery in between, is status epilepticus, where the brain begins consuming itself metabolically. Beyond about five minutes it stops being self-limiting and becomes an emergency.
