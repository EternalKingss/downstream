    Shared node · Decompensating

    The body compensates

    Catecholamines buy time by making the remaining circulation work harder and cover less ground.

    What is actually happening

    Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch detect falling pressure and trigger a sympathetic surge. Heart rate and contractility rise, arterioles constrict in skin, gut, and muscle, and veins constrict to squeeze stored blood back into circulation. Blood is redistributed to brain and heart at the expense of everything else.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the automatic response to any fall in perfusion pressure, regardless of cause.

    What you see

    • Tachycardia, usually the earliest measurable sign
    • Pale, cool, clammy, sometimes mottled skin
    • Narrowing pulse pressure as diastolic climbs and systolic falls
    • Anxiety, agitation, a sense of impending doom
    • Nausea and reduced bowel sounds as the gut is sacrificed
    • A blood pressure that stays stubbornly, misleadingly normal
    The trap

    This is the stage where the patient looks compensated and the numbers look acceptable, and it is the stage where intervention actually works. It is also finite. Some patients cannot mount it at all: those on beta blockers, those with spinal cord injury above the sympathetic outflow, older patients, and well-conditioned athletes with low resting rates. A sick patient who is not tachycardic is more worrying, not less.
