    Core temperature falls

    Enzyme systems slow down, and eventually the mechanisms meant to fix it stop working too.

    What is actually happening

    Heat loss exceeds heat production. The body vasoconstricts and shivers to compensate, but both have limits. As the core cools, every enzyme-driven process slows: clotting, cardiac conduction, consciousness, and metabolism.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows cold exposure, immersion, trauma with exposure, alcohol, hypoglycemia, hypothyroidism, spinal cord injury, and simply lying on a cold floor for hours, which is the commonest version in older adults.

    What you see

    • Mild, 32 to 35 degrees: shivering, clumsiness, confusion, mumbling
    • Moderate, 28 to 32: shivering stops, muscles stiffen, consciousness falls, pulse and breathing slow
    • Severe, below 28: unresponsive, barely detectable pulse and breathing, high risk of ventricular fibrillation
    • Cold, pale, sometimes paradoxically undressed patients who felt hot as vasoconstriction failed
    The trap

    Two things. First, a patient who has stopped shivering has deteriorated, not improved. Second, a severely hypothermic heart is exquisitely irritable, and rough handling can precipitate ventricular fibrillation, so these patients are moved gently and horizontally. Hypothermia is also protective of the brain, which is why resuscitation is continued far longer than normal and why nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.
