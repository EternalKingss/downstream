    Breathing stops

    Ventilation ceases while a pulse is still present.

    What is actually happening

    Respiratory effort ends, either because the drive to breathe has failed or because the muscles can no longer generate it. Circulation continues for a few minutes on already-oxygenated blood, then fails.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the terminal point of respiratory fatigue, central respiratory depression, or complete airway obstruction.

    What you see

    • No chest rise, no air movement, no breath sounds
    • A palpable pulse that is often still present initially
    • Deepening cyanosis
    • Bradycardia developing, particularly rapidly in children
    The trap

    This is the most salvageable emergency you will attend. A patient in respiratory arrest with a pulse needs ventilation and will very often recover completely. If you wait, it becomes cardiac arrest, and survival drops off a cliff. In children this progression is the usual route to arrest, which is why paediatric resuscitation is airway and breathing first.
