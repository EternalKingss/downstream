    Tissue oxygen falls

    Cells stop getting the oxygen they need to make energy.

    What is actually happening

    Cells make ATP by aerobic metabolism, which requires oxygen as the final electron acceptor. Without it they switch to anaerobic metabolism, which produces roughly a sixteenth as much energy per glucose molecule and generates lactic acid as a byproduct. High-demand tissue fails first.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    There are only four ways to arrive here, and every respiratory and circulatory emergency is one of them: not enough oxygen going in, not enough gas exchange happening, not enough blood carrying it, or not enough flow delivering it.

    What you see

    • Anxiety and restlessness, often before anything else and often mistaken for panic
    • Falling SpO2, though this is a late indicator because of the shape of the oxygen dissociation curve
    • Tachycardia and tachypnea as the body tries to move more oxygen faster
    • Cyanosis, which requires a substantial amount of desaturated hemoglobin and is therefore also late
    • Deteriorating level of consciousness as the brain runs short
    The trap

    Restlessness and combativeness are hypoxia until proven otherwise. Treating an agitated hypoxic patient as a behavioural problem instead of a respiratory one is a classic and lethal error.
