    Brain blood flow falls

    The brain is at the top of the circuit and it notices first.

    What is actually happening

    Cerebral perfusion pressure is mean arterial pressure minus intracranial pressure. The brain autoregulates flow across a wide range of pressures, but below roughly a mean arterial pressure of 60 that autoregulation fails and flow becomes directly dependent on systemic pressure.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows from anything that lowers cardiac output or mean arterial pressure, and from anything that raises intracranial pressure.

    What you see

    • Anxiety, restlessness, confusion
    • Lightheadedness on sitting or standing
    • Visual greying, tunnel vision, then syncope
    • Progressive fall in level of consciousness if flow does not recover
    The trap

    This is why hypotension is so devastating in a head-injured patient. Their intracranial pressure is already raised, so their perfusion pressure is already squeezed from one side. A single episode of hypotension in traumatic brain injury measurably worsens outcome, which is why the pressure target in those patients is deliberately higher.
