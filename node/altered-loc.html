    Level of consciousness drops

    The brain is the first organ to complain and the first to shut down.

    What is actually happening

    The brain consumes about twenty percent of the body's oxygen while making up two percent of its mass, and it stores essentially no reserve. Any interruption to its oxygen or glucose supply shows up in behaviour within seconds to minutes.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It sits downstream of almost everything: hypoxia, hypercapnia, hypoglycemia, hypoperfusion, toxins, raised intracranial pressure, and seizure activity all converge here.

    What you see

    • Anxiety and restlessness first, which is easy to misread
    • Confusion, disorientation, and inappropriate answers
    • Combativeness, which is often the last thing before collapse
    • Progressive drowsiness, then response only to voice, then only to pain, then nothing
    • Loss of airway reflexes and airway tone, which creates a second, mechanical problem on top of the first
    The trap

    This node is the single most important convergence point in prehospital medicine, and precisely because so many things lead here it tells you almost nothing on its own about the cause. Its value is as an alarm and as a severity marker, not as a diagnosis.
