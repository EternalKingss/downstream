    Carbon dioxide accumulates

    CO2 builds up because it isn't being blown off fast enough.

    What is actually happening

    CO2 is produced continuously by metabolism and cleared only by ventilation. When alveolar ventilation drops, CO2 accumulates and combines with water to form carbonic acid, dropping blood pH. This is respiratory acidosis.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    CO2 clearance depends almost entirely on how much air actually reaches the alveoli each minute. Slow breathing, shallow breathing, and trapped air all reduce that, even when the rate looks normal or high.

    What you see

    • Headache, flushing, and warm peripheries from CO2-driven vasodilation
    • Drowsiness progressing to confusion, then unresponsiveness
    • Bounding pulse
    • In severe cases, a flapping tremor of the outstretched hands
    The trap

    CO2 diffuses about twenty times more easily than oxygen, so in most lung disease hypoxia arrives well before hypercapnia. A rising CO2 in a respiratory patient usually means ventilation itself is failing, which is a much later and more serious stage than hypoxia alone.
