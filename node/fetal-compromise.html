    The fetus is compromised

    Placental blood flow falls, and the fetus has no reserve of its own.

    What is actually happening

    The fetus depends entirely on maternal blood flow through the placenta. That circulation has no autoregulation, so it falls directly with maternal blood pressure or cardiac output. It is also the first vascular bed sacrificed when the mother compensates for blood loss.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows maternal hemorrhage, maternal hypoxia, maternal hypotension including from lying supine, placental abruption, cord compression or prolapse, and uterine trauma.

    What you see

    • Reduced fetal movements reported by the mother
    • Abnormal fetal heart rate if you can assess it
    • Maternal signs that may still look entirely normal
    • Vaginal bleeding or a rigid tender uterus
    The trap

    The fetus is compromised long before the mother shows any sign, because maternal compensation works by shutting down the uterine circulation first. A pregnant trauma patient with completely normal vital signs may already have a distressed fetus. The corollary is the single most useful principle in obstetric emergencies: resuscitate the mother and you resuscitate the fetus. Oxygen, left lateral tilt, and volume for her are the best fetal treatment available to you.

    Cascades that reach here

    Different starting points, identical endpoint. This is what makes the node worth learning once instead of eleven times.
