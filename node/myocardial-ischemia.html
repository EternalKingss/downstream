    Heart muscle starves

    The myocardium's oxygen demand exceeds what its own arteries can supply.

    What is actually happening

    Cardiac muscle already extracts around seventy percent of the oxygen in blood passing through it at rest, so it cannot compensate by extracting more. Its only response to increased demand is increased flow. When flow is limited by a narrowed or blocked coronary artery, the muscle switches to anaerobic metabolism, lactate accumulates, and the tissue signals pain.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    Either supply drops, from plaque rupture, thrombus, spasm, or profound hypotension, or demand climbs, from tachycardia, hypertension, or exertion. Most real presentations are a bit of both.

    What you see

    • Chest discomfort described as pressure, tightness, heaviness, or a band, often not as pain at all
    • Radiation to jaw, neck, shoulders, arms, or between the shoulder blades
    • Sweating, nausea, and shortness of breath
    • A sense of impending doom, which is a genuine clinical finding and not just anxiety
    • Presentations without any chest pain in women, older adults, and people with diabetes
    The trap

    Ischemic muscle is irritable muscle, and irritable muscle generates lethal rhythms. The greatest risk in the first hour of an infarct is not pump failure, it is ventricular fibrillation. That is why the AED goes on the patient early and why you do not leave them to walk to the stretcher.
