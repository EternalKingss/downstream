    Work of breathing rises

    Breathing stops being free and starts costing real energy.

    What is actually happening

    Quiet breathing normally uses about three percent of your total oxygen consumption. When resistance or stiffness increases, the respiratory muscles have to generate far more pressure for the same tidal volume. The body recruits accessory muscles in the neck, shoulders, and abdomen to help.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    Any obstruction to flow, any loss of lung compliance, and any restriction of chest movement forces the same compensation. The body's only option is to push harder.

    What you see

    • Accessory muscle use: sternocleidomastoid standing out, shoulders rising with each breath
    • Intercostal, subcostal, and suprasternal retractions, most obvious in children
    • Tripod positioning, hands braced on knees or a table to fix the shoulder girdle
    • Nasal flaring in infants and small children
    • Speaking in short phrases, then single words, then not at all
    The trap

    This is a compensation, not a failure. A patient working hard is still winning. The dangerous moment is when the work suddenly decreases without treatment, because that usually means they have run out of fuel rather than gotten better.
