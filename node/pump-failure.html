    Cardiac output falls

    The heart is still beating but no longer moving enough blood.

    What is actually happening

    Stroke volume collapses because the muscle cannot contract effectively, because the ventricle cannot fill, or because the valves are not directing flow. Cardiac output falls even though electrical activity and heart rate may look entirely normal on a monitor.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It sits downstream of infarction, of arrhythmia, of tamponade, of tension pneumothorax, and of anything that damages muscle or prevents filling.

    What you see

    • Hypotension with cool peripheries
    • Blood backing up behind the failing side: crackles and breathlessness for the left, distended neck veins and swollen legs for the right
    • Tachycardia attempting to compensate for a stroke volume that will not recover
    • Falling level of consciousness
    The trap

    The monitor shows electricity, not output. A perfect-looking rhythm with no pulse is pulseless electrical activity, and it means the problem is mechanical. Defibrillation cannot fix a mechanical problem. Go looking for the cause instead.
