    Heart rate climbs

    The pump speeds up to move the same oxygen more often.

    What is actually happening

    Chemoreceptors and baroreceptors detect falling oxygen or falling pressure and trigger a sympathetic response. Cardiac output is heart rate multiplied by stroke volume, so when stroke volume or oxygen content falls, raising rate is the fastest available compensation.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the body's default answer to almost every oxygen delivery problem, whether the cause is respiratory, haemorrhagic, or distributive.

    What you see

    • Rising pulse rate, often the earliest measurable change in a deteriorating patient
    • Narrowing pulse pressure if the cause is volume loss
    • Pale, cool, clammy skin from accompanying peripheral vasoconstriction
    The trap

    Tachycardia is nonspecific but it is rarely nothing. More importantly, its absence in a sick patient is alarming. A patient on beta blockers, a well-conditioned athlete, or a patient with a spinal injury may not be able to mount this response at all.
