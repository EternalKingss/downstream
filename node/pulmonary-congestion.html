    Fluid backs up into the lungs

    The left heart can't clear what the lungs deliver, so pressure rises behind it and fluid floods the alveoli.

    What is actually happening

    When the left ventricle fails to empty, pressure rises in the left atrium and back through the pulmonary veins into the pulmonary capillaries. Once hydrostatic pressure there exceeds the oncotic pressure holding fluid in, plasma is forced out into the interstitium and then into the alveoli themselves.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the backward consequence of left-sided pump failure from any cause: infarction, arrhythmia, valve failure, or a hypertensive crisis the ventricle cannot pump against.

    What you see

    • Severe breathlessness, dramatically worse lying flat
    • Fine crackles beginning at the bases and rising as it worsens
    • Frothy sputum, sometimes pink from red cells forced across with the fluid
    • Wheeze in some patients, which is genuinely hard to distinguish from asthma
    • Anxiety, sweating, and a patient who will not tolerate being laid down
    The trap

    Cardiac wheeze exists and it fools people constantly. A wheezing patient with a cardiac history, crackles at the bases, orthopnea, and swollen ankles is not having an asthma attack, and salbutamol will only add tachycardia to a heart that cannot afford it.
