    Plasma leaks out through damaged tissue

    Burned or inflamed tissue leaks fluid out of the circulation for hours.

    What is actually happening

    Heat and inflammatory mediators damage capillary walls, so plasma escapes into the interstitium and out through the burned surface. In a large burn this becomes a body-wide capillary leak rather than a local one. Losses are greatest in the first eight hours and continue for a day or more.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows burns of significant surface area, severe inflammation, sepsis, and anaphylaxis.

    What you see

    • Progressive swelling of the burned area and often beyond it
    • Rising heart rate over hours in a patient who initially seemed stable
    • Falling urine output
    • Thirst
    • Hypotension developing hours after the injury rather than at the time
    The trap

    Burn shock is not immediate. A patient with a large burn who is hemodynamically normal at the roadside is not reassuring, they are simply early. If a burn patient is shocked in the first minutes, suspect another injury such as bleeding or a tension pneumothorax, because the burn itself has not had time to do it yet.
