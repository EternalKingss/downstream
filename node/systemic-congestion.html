    Fluid backs up into the body

    The right heart can't clear venous return, so pressure rises throughout the venous system.

    What is actually happening

    When the right ventricle fails, blood backs up into the venae cavae and the systemic veins. Venous pressure rises, and fluid is forced out of capillaries into dependent tissue and into the abdomen.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows right ventricular infarction, chronic lung disease that has raised pulmonary pressures over years, pulmonary embolism, and long-standing left heart failure that has eventually overloaded the right.

    What you see

    • Distended neck veins, visible with the patient sitting up
    • Swollen ankles and legs, or swelling over the sacrum in someone bed-bound
    • Enlarged tender liver and abdominal distension
    • Lungs that are often clear, which surprises people
    The trap

    Right-sided failure makes the patient preload dependent, because a failing right ventricle needs a high filling pressure to produce any output at all. Anything that drops preload, including nitroglycerin and aggressive positive pressure ventilation, can cause abrupt collapse.

    Cascades that reach here

    Different starting points, identical endpoint. This is what makes the node worth learning once instead of eleven times.
