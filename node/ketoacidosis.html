    Fat breakdown acidifies the blood

    Without insulin the body burns fat for fuel, and the byproducts are acids.

    What is actually happening

    Insulin does two things: it lets glucose into cells and it suppresses fat breakdown. Without it, cells starve despite high blood glucose, so the body mobilises fat. The liver converts fatty acids into ketone bodies, which are usable fuel but are also acids. They accumulate faster than they can be buffered and blood pH falls.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows absolute insulin deficiency, which means type 1 diabetes almost exclusively: a missed dose, a pump failure, or an infection raising insulin requirement beyond what was given.

    What you see

    • Deep, rapid, sighing respirations as the lungs try to blow off carbon dioxide to compensate
    • A sweet or fruity smell on the breath from acetone
    • Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain that can mimic a surgical abdomen
    • Drowsiness progressing to unresponsiveness
    • Dehydration from the accompanying osmotic diuresis
    The trap

    The compensatory hyperventilation is doing essential work. Those deep sighing breaths are not distress, they are the only thing keeping the pH survivable. If that respiratory effort falls, either from exhaustion or from sedation, the pH crashes. Never suppress or take over this patient's breathing without understanding that you must then match their minute volume.
