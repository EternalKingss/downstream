    Compensation fails

    The reserves run out and blood pressure finally falls.

    What is actually happening

    Sympathetic mechanisms have a ceiling. Prolonged hypoperfusion produces acidosis, and acidosis blunts the response of vascular smooth muscle and myocardium to catecholamines. The vasoconstriction that was holding pressure up begins to fail, and pressure drops.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the endpoint of any sustained hypoperfusion that has not been corrected.

    What you see

    • Falling systolic blood pressure, which in an adult usually means around thirty percent of blood volume is already gone
    • Weak, thready peripheral pulses, then loss of radial pulse entirely
    • Deteriorating level of consciousness
    • Mottled, cold skin and capillary refill well beyond three seconds
    • Sometimes a paradoxical fall in heart rate as a very late and very bad sign
    The trap

    Falling blood pressure is not the start of shock, it is the announcement that shock has been present and unaddressed for some time. If you wait for hypotension to diagnose shock, you have waited too long. In children this stage arrives late and then collapses fast, which is why paediatric hypotension is treated as near-arrest.
