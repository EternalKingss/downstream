    Electrical injury

    Current travels through the body along the path of least resistance, cooking tissue on the way and potentially stopping the heart.

    In one breath

    What you can see is almost irrelevant. Two small marks on the skin can conceal a limb full of destroyed muscle, because the damage happens along the internal path the current took. And if that path crossed the chest, the heart may stop.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Current enters the body
    Voltage, current, path, duration

    Severity depends on voltage, current, resistance of the tissue, the path taken, and contact duration. Alternating current, as in household supply, causes tetanic muscle contraction that prevents the patient letting go, which massively extends contact time. Direct current tends to throw them clear.

    Scene safety absolutely first EMR

    Do not approach until the supply is confirmed isolated by someone qualified. For high voltage, maintain a large exclusion zone and wait for the utility company. Rescuers who become second patients are a recurring feature of these incidents.

    It follows the path of least resistance
    Nerves, vessels, muscle

    Nerve, blood, and muscle conduct well; bone and fat resist and generate heat instead. Hand-to-hand and hand-to-opposite-foot paths cross the chest and are the dangerous ones. Hand-to-same-foot may spare the heart entirely.

    The heart can be stopped
    VF, asystole, or later arrhythmia

    Current crossing the chest can depolarise the myocardium during its vulnerable period and cause ventricular fibrillation. Low-voltage alternating current characteristically causes VF; high-voltage direct current and lightning more often cause asystole, from which the heart may restart spontaneously while respiration does not.

    Resuscitate persistently EMR

    Electrical arrest occurs in otherwise healthy people with healthy hearts, and outcomes are far better than most arrests. In lightning strike with multiple casualties, triage is reversed: treat the apparently dead first, because those who are breathing will usually survive without you.

    Transport everyone with a significant exposure EMR

    Delayed arrhythmias occur. Anyone with a current path across the chest, loss of consciousness, high voltage, or any abnormality needs monitoring even if they feel completely well.

    Breathing can stop independently
    Tetany or brainstem effect

    Current can paralyse the respiratory muscles through tetanic contraction, or suppress the brainstem centre directly. Crucially, breathing often does not restart when the heart does, so a patient can have a pulse and remain apneic. Prolonged ventilation alone saves these patients.

    shared node → Breathing stops

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration, with ventilation if apneic.

    Keep ventilating even after a pulse returns EMR

    Respiratory arrest frequently outlasts cardiac arrest here. A patient with a pulse who is not breathing needs continued ventilation, sometimes for a prolonged period, and will often recover fully.

    Deep tissue is cooked
    Surface massively understates it

    Resistance converts electrical energy to heat, and the highest resistance tissues along the path get the hottest. Muscle deep to normal-looking skin can be destroyed over a long track. The visible entry and exit wounds are the least important part of the injury.

    anatomy → Soft tissue and wounds

    Assume the injury is far worse than it looks EMR

    Look for entry and exit wounds and think about what lies on the line between them. Two small marks can mean a destroyed forearm. Do not estimate severity from surface area.

    Destroyed muscle floods the circulation
    Potassium and myoglobin

    Extensive deep muscle necrosis releases potassium, myoglobin, and acid. Arrhythmia and renal failure follow, and these patients need aggressive fluid resuscitation calibrated to the internal damage, not the visible burn area.

    shared node → Damaged muscle releases its contents
    Limbs swell inside their fascia
    Compartment syndrome

    Deep muscle injury and oedema raise compartment pressure. Electrical injury is one of the commonest causes of compartment syndrome requiring fasciotomy.

    shared node → Pressure rises inside a closed compartment
    Secondary injuries
    Falls and violent contraction

    Patients are thrown clear, fall from height, or fracture bones and dislocate joints purely from the violence of the muscle contraction. Posterior shoulder dislocation and spinal fractures from tetany are well described.

    Spinal precautions where mechanism warrants EMR

    Falls and tetanic contractions cause spinal fractures. Consider it in any high-voltage injury or fall from height.

    Stop and escalate
    • Any loss of consciousness
    • High voltage exposure, above 1000 volts
    • Current path crossing the chest
    • Dark urine, suggesting myoglobin
    • Any arrhythmia
    • Entry and exit wounds far apart
    • Fall from height or being thrown
    • Lightning strike
    • Wet contact, which reduces skin resistance dramatically

    Telling it apart

    Not Thermal burns?

    Thermal burn severity correlates with visible area. Electrical injury does not, at all. Fluid requirements in electrical injury are calculated on the internal damage and are usually far higher than the visible burn suggests.

    Not Lightning strike?

    Massive current for a very brief duration. Causes asystole rather than VF, often with spontaneous cardiac restart, prolonged apnea, and a characteristic branching skin pattern. Deep tissue damage is usually less than industrial electrocution because the duration is so short.

    Not Simple arc flash burn?

    An arc flash burns the surface without current passing through the body, so it behaves like a thermal burn without the internal injuries or arrhythmia risk.
