    Condition · Chest wall and diaphragm

    Rib fractures

    Pain that stops the patient breathing deeply, which is far more dangerous than the broken bone itself.

    In one breath

    The fracture heals on its own. What harms the patient is that breathing hurts, so they breathe shallowly and stop coughing, secretions accumulate, small airways collapse, and pneumonia follows. In an older patient this chain is genuinely lethal.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    One or more ribs break
    Blunt force, or minor trauma if elderly

    Substantial force in a young person, but often trivial force in an older adult with osteoporotic bone. Ribs one to three take enormous energy to break and imply major trauma; ribs nine to twelve overlie the liver, spleen, and kidneys, so fractures there raise the question of abdominal injury.

    anatomy → Chest wall and diaphragm
    Movement of the chest hurts
    Worst on inspiration and coughing

    The fracture ends move against each other and against the pleura with every breath. This is severe, sharp, and reliably reproduced by exactly the movements the patient most needs to make.

    Analgesia is a respiratory intervention PCP

    The single most important treatment, and it is not about comfort. A patient with adequate pain control breathes deeply and coughs, which prevents the entire downstream cascade. Undertreated rib fracture pain in an older adult is a genuine cause of death.

    The patient breathes shallowly
    And stops coughing

    Voluntary and involuntary splinting reduces tidal volume, and the patient suppresses the cough because it is agonising. This is the pivot point of the whole condition: everything downstream follows from a patient who will not take a deep breath.

    Never strap or bind the chest EMR

    Old teaching that has been abandoned for good reason. Binding restricts ventilation of the whole side and directly causes the collapse and pneumonia you are trying to avoid.

    Position of comfort, usually upright EMR

    Improves mechanics and drainage. Some patients prefer lying on the injured side to splint it, which is acceptable if it lets them breathe more deeply overall.

    Small airways collapse
    No deep breaths to reopen them

    Alveoli and small airways collapse during normal shallow breathing and are normally reopened by periodic deep breaths and sighs. Without them, regions of lung stay collapsed, which is atelectasis.

    Encourage deep breathing and coughing EMR

    Counterintuitive when it hurts, but it is the treatment. A pillow hugged against the chest wall lets patients splint the fracture with their own hands while still taking a deep breath.

    Secretions accumulate
    The cough was the clearance mechanism

    A suppressed cough means mucus is not cleared. It pools in dependent and collapsed regions and provides an excellent culture medium.

    Collapsed lung is perfused but not ventilated
    Progressive hypoxia

    Blood flows past collapsed regions and returns unoxygenated. Hypoxia develops gradually over hours to days rather than immediately, which is why these patients are often discharged and then deteriorate.

    shared node → Ventilation and perfusion stop matching

    Oxygen EMR

    Titrate to target. Reassess repeatedly, because the hypoxia here is progressive rather than immediate.

    Pneumonia develops
    The actual cause of death

    Retained secretions in collapsed lung become infected. In patients over 65, multiple rib fractures carry a mortality that is far higher than most people expect, and the mechanism is almost always this one. Each additional fractured rib in an older adult measurably increases mortality.

    Take elderly rib fractures seriously EMR

    An older adult with two or three fractured ribs from a standing fall is not a minor injury. They need assessment, analgesia, and often admission, and they are frequently undertriaged.

    Something underneath may be injured
    Lung, liver, spleen, vessels

    The force that broke the rib went somewhere. Pneumothorax, hemothorax, pulmonary contusion, and injury to liver, spleen, or kidney all travel with rib fractures, and any of them may matter more than the fracture.

    Look hard for what else is injured EMR

    Check breath sounds bilaterally, palpate the abdomen, and reassess. Lower rib fractures with abdominal tenderness means solid organ injury until proven otherwise.

    Stop and escalate
    • Age over 65 with multiple fractures
    • Any pre-existing lung disease
    • Falling saturation, which may develop over hours
    • Reduced or absent breath sounds on one side
    • Abdominal tenderness with lower rib fractures
    • Fractures of the first three ribs, indicating major force
    • Paradoxical chest movement, which is flail chest
    • A patient who cannot take a deep breath at all

    Telling it apart

    Not Flail chest?

    Flail chest has a segment moving paradoxically, requiring at least three adjacent ribs broken in two places each. Simple fractures hurt just as much but do not disrupt the mechanics of the chest wall.

    Not Pneumothorax?

    Fractured ribs cause pneumothoraces. Unilateral reduced breath sounds with hyperresonance means the fracture has punctured the lung.

    Not Musculoskeletal chest wall pain?

    Both are reproducible on palpation. In practice they are managed similarly in the field, and the distinction is made on imaging.
