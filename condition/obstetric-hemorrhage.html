    Condition · Uterus and placenta, Maternal physiology

    Obstetric hemorrhage

    The uterus at term takes a fifth of cardiac output, so obstetric bleeding empties a patient faster than almost any other injury.

    In one breath

    Nearly a litre a minute flows through the term uterus. When that circuit opens, blood loss is extraordinary. And because pregnancy expands blood volume by up to half, the patient compensates beautifully and then collapses without much warning.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    The bleeding source opens
    Abruption, praevia, atony, or trauma

    Before delivery: placental abruption, where the placenta separates early and bleeds behind itself, often concealed; or placenta praevia, where it lies over the cervix and bleeds painlessly. After delivery: uterine atony, retained placenta, or genital tract trauma. Ectopic pregnancy bleeds into the abdomen in early pregnancy.

    anatomy → Uterus and placenta

    Never perform a vaginal examination EMR

    In third-trimester bleeding, examination can provoke catastrophic hemorrhage if there is a placenta praevia. Look externally, count pads, and transport.

    The circuit is enormous
    700 to 900 mL per minute at term

    Uterine blood flow at term approaches a fifth of cardiac output. A bleeding placental bed is closer to an arterial bleed than to typical venous oozing, and the volumes lost per minute are unlike anything else in prehospital care.

    Bleeding may be hidden
    Abruption especially

    In placental abruption, blood collects behind the placenta and may never appear externally. The classic presentation is a rigid, painful, tender uterus with shock and little or no visible blood. Never estimate obstetric blood loss from what you can see.

    shared node → Bleeding you cannot see

    Assume the loss is greater than you can see EMR

    Abruption conceals blood behind the placenta, and postpartum loss soaks into bedding and is chronically underestimated. Treat as significant hemorrhage regardless of visible volume.

    Pregnancy masks the shock
    1200 to 1500 mL before signs

    Blood volume rises 30 to 50 percent, resting heart rate is already elevated, and blood pressure normally runs low. A pregnant patient can lose well over a litre with vital signs that look almost normal. Do not be reassured.

    anatomy → Maternal physiology

    Left lateral tilt, always EMR

    After twenty weeks, supine positioning compresses the vena cava and can reduce cardiac output by a third in a patient who cannot spare it. Tilt left or manually displace the uterus to the left.

    The uterine circulation is shut down first
    Fetus suffers before mother shows signs

    Maternal compensation preserves brain and heart by constricting other beds, and the uterine circulation is among the first sacrificed. It has no autoregulation, so fetal perfusion falls directly with it. The fetus is in trouble long before the mother's numbers move.

    shared node → The fetus is compromised

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration. Maternal oxygenation is the most direct fetal resuscitation available to you.

    After delivery, the uterus fails to contract
    The commonest postpartum cause

    The placental site is closed by muscular contraction rather than by clotting. If the uterus stays soft and boggy, those vessels stay open. Risk factors include prolonged labour, multiple pregnancy, large baby, and high parity.

    Fundal massage for postpartum bleeding EMR

    Firm circular massage of the fundus through the abdominal wall until it becomes hard. This physically compresses the bleeding vessels and it works. It is the single most effective thing an EMR can do in obstetric hemorrhage.

    Put the baby to the breast if delivered EMR

    Suckling releases oxytocin, which stimulates uterine contraction. Free, immediate, and physiologically genuine.

    Compensation collapses
    Abruptly, and late

    When the expanded volume is exhausted, deterioration is fast. Coagulopathy develops readily in abruption, because placental tissue entering the circulation triggers widespread clotting that consumes the factors needed to stop the bleed.

    shared node → Compensation fails

    Warm, horizontal, rapid transport, pre-alert EMR

    These patients need blood, oxytocic drugs, and often surgery. A pre-alert lets the obstetric and blood bank teams be ready, and it changes outcomes.

    Stop and escalate
    • Any third-trimester bleeding
    • Rigid, tender, painful uterus, suggesting abruption
    • Shock with little visible blood
    • Soft boggy uterus after delivery
    • Estimated loss over 500 mL, or any loss with symptoms
    • Abdominal pain in early pregnancy with shoulder tip pain, suggesting ruptured ectopic
    • Heart rate rising in a pregnant patient even with a normal blood pressure
    • Reduced fetal movements

    Telling it apart

    Not Placenta praevia?

    Painless bright red bleeding with a soft uterus. Abruption is painful with a rigid tender uterus and often concealed blood.

    Not Ectopic pregnancy?

    Early pregnancy, often before the pregnancy is known, with abdominal pain and shock. Shoulder tip pain from blood irritating the diaphragm. Consider it in every woman of childbearing age with abdominal pain and shock.

    Not Uterine rupture?

    Rare and catastrophic, usually in a patient with a previous caesarean scar. Sudden severe pain, cessation of contractions, and rapid shock.
