    Condition · Cerebrum, Pancreas

    Hypoglycemia

    Blood glucose falls below what the brain needs, and the brain has no reserve of its own.

    In one breath

    The brain cannot burn fat and stores almost no glycogen, so it fails within minutes of the glucose supply falling. It imitates stroke, intoxication, and psychiatric emergency perfectly, and it reverses completely in fifteen minutes. That combination is why you check a sugar on everyone.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Glucose supply falls short of demand
    Too much insulin, too little food

    Most often insulin or a sulfonylurea taken with a missed meal, unusual exertion, or alcohol. Alcohol matters twice over, because it suppresses the liver's ability to manufacture new glucose. Sepsis, liver failure, and adrenal insufficiency also cause it.

    anatomy → Pancreas

    Recheck and then feed them properly EMR

    Recheck at fifteen minutes. Once corrected, give complex carbohydrate and protein, because fast sugar alone wears off within an hour or two and they will fall again.

    Decide carefully about transport EMR

    A straightforward insulin episode with a clear cause and someone at home may be safe to leave. A sulfonylurea, alcohol, a first episode, no obvious cause, or a patient living alone all need hospital, because sulfonylureas and alcohol both outlast your treatment.

    The body fights back
    Adrenaline, glucagon, cortisol

    Glucagon and adrenaline are released to mobilise stored glucose. The adrenaline surge is what produces the classic early warning signs, and importantly they are a symptom of the defence rather than of the deficiency itself.

    anatomy → Adrenal glands
    Sympathetic warning signs appear
    Sweating, tremor, hunger, anxiety

    Sweating, tremor, palpitations, pallor, hunger, and anxiety. These are the symptoms most diabetics recognise as their cue to eat. They are absent in patients on beta blockers and in long-standing diabetics with hypoglycemia unawareness, who go straight to confusion with no warning at all.

    shared node → The body compensates
    The brain runs out of fuel
    Confusion, slurred speech, odd behaviour

    Below roughly 3 mmol/L, cortical function fails. Confusion, difficulty concentrating, slurred speech, and behaviour that is out of character. Aggression is common and is the reason these patients are so often treated as behavioural rather than medical.

    shared node → The brain runs out of fuel

    Oral glucose EMR

    15 to 25 grams of gel between cheek and gum, or swallowed. Reverses the entire cascade. Requires an intact gag reflex and safe swallow, without exception.

    Focal neurological signs can appear
    A perfect stroke mimic

    Hypoglycemia can produce one-sided weakness, facial droop, and dysphasia indistinguishable from a stroke. It resolves completely with glucose. This single fact is why blood glucose is checked before any stroke pathway is activated, anywhere.

    anatomy → Cerebrum

    Check the glucose, always EMR

    On every patient with altered mental status, every seizure, every stroke presentation, every collapse, every intoxicated patient, every aggressive patient. It takes seconds and it is the cheapest diagnosis in medicine.

    Seizure and unresponsiveness
    Below about 2 mmol/L

    Neurons deprived of substrate become unstable and then fail. Seizure activity itself consumes still more glucose, deepening the deficit and creating a loop.

    shared node → Neurons fire uncontrollably
    The airway is lost
    Which blocks the obvious treatment

    An unresponsive patient cannot swallow, which rules out the oral glucose that would fix everything. This is exactly the point at which the condition stops being trivially treatable at EMR level.

    shared node → Airway stops protecting itself

    Oxygen EMR

    If hypoxic or unresponsive. Does not treat the cause but supports a brain that is already substrate-starved.

    Nothing by mouth if consciousness is reduced EMR

    Gel in the mouth of a patient who cannot swallow causes aspiration. If they cannot protect their airway, they need IV glucose or glucagon and you should escalate.

    Permanent injury develops
    Prolonged severe hypoglycemia

    Sustained severe hypoglycemia causes permanent neuronal death. Duration matters more than depth, which is why treating quickly matters and why a patient who does not recover after correction may have been down too long.

    Stop and escalate
    • Sulfonylurea medication such as gliclazide or glyburide, which recurs for many hours
    • Alcohol involved
    • Failure to improve within fifteen minutes of correction
    • First episode with no explanation
    • Living alone with no one to observe them
    • Seizure or prolonged unresponsiveness
    • Repeated episodes on the same day

    Telling it apart

    Not Stroke?

    Hypoglycemia can produce identical focal deficits and resolves completely with glucose. Check it before activating any stroke pathway.

    Not Intoxication?

    Slurred speech, poor coordination, and odd behaviour look the same. Alcohol also causes hypoglycemia directly, so they frequently coexist. Check the sugar on every intoxicated patient.

    Not Behavioural emergency?

    Aggression and agitation are classic presentations of low sugar. Restraining a hypoglycemic patient instead of treating them is a well-documented and avoidable harm.

    Not Diabetic ketoacidosis?

    Opposite ends of the same disease. DKA develops over days with dehydration, deep sighing breathing, and a fruity smell. Hypoglycemia develops over minutes to hours with sweating and tremor.
