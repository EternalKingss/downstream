    Condition · Temperature regulation

    Hypothermia

    Core temperature falls until enzyme systems slow and the mechanisms meant to correct it stop working.

    In one breath

    Heat loss beats heat production. The body vasoconstricts and shivers until it cannot, and then everything slows: clotting, conduction, consciousness. The heart becomes dangerously irritable, which is why these patients are handled as if they were made of glass.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Heat loss exceeds production
    Wet and wind multiply it

    Radiation, conduction, convection, and evaporation. Wet clothing conducts heat away roughly twenty-five times faster than dry, and wind strips the warm layer next to the skin. Alberta cases are frequently not dramatic exposure but an older person who fell and lay on a floor overnight, or an intoxicated patient outdoors.

    anatomy → Temperature regulation

    Remove wet clothing and insulate EMR

    Cut clothing off rather than manoeuvring limbs. Dry them, insulate including the head, and provide a vapour barrier. Stopping further loss matters more than adding heat.

    The body compensates
    Vasoconstriction and shivering

    Peripheral vasoconstriction reduces surface heat loss, and shivering generates heat through muscular work, raising metabolic rate several-fold. Both are effective and both are finite. Alcohol defeats the first by causing vasodilation, and exhaustion or glucose depletion defeats the second.

    Warm the trunk, not the limbs EMR

    Active warming applied to the torso, axillae, and groin. Warming cold limbs first returns cold acidotic blood to the heart and can drop the core temperature further, which is afterdrop.

    Check glucose and look for the cause EMR

    Hypoglycemia, alcohol, hypothyroidism, sepsis, spinal cord injury, and stroke all cause or contribute to hypothermia. In an older patient found cold at home, the cold is often the consequence of something else.

    Mild hypothermia, 32 to 35
    Shivering, clumsy, confused

    Vigorous shivering, clumsiness, poor coordination, mumbling speech, and impaired judgement. Patients at this stage make decisions that worsen their situation, which is part of why they deteriorate.

    Shivering stops
    Below about 32 degrees

    The single most important transition. Shivering ceases below roughly 32 degrees, and to an observer the patient appears calmer. They are not improving; they have lost their main heat-generating mechanism. Muscles become rigid, consciousness falls, and paradoxical undressing may occur as failing vasoconstriction gives a false sensation of heat.

    The heart becomes irritable
    Handling can trigger VF

    Cold myocardium conducts abnormally and is highly prone to ventricular fibrillation. Rough handling, sudden movement, or jolting can precipitate it. This is why severely hypothermic patients are moved horizontally, gently, and as little as possible.

    anatomy → Conduction system

    Handle them extremely gently EMR

    The most important rule. Keep them horizontal, avoid jolting, avoid unnecessary movement, and do not let them walk or exert themselves. Rough handling of a severely hypothermic patient can cause ventricular fibrillation.

    Clotting fails
    Enzymes are temperature-dependent

    Clotting factors are enzymes and work poorly when cold. In a trauma patient this closes the lethal triad of hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy, each worsening the others. It is why a warm trauma patient does better than a cold one with identical injuries.

    Severe hypothermia, below 28
    Looks dead but isn't

    Unresponsive, muscles rigid, pupils may be fixed and dilated, pulse and respiration so slow and shallow as to be undetectable. Metabolic demand is very low, so the brain tolerates this state remarkably well. Full neurological recovery has occurred after prolonged severe hypothermia.

    Oxygen EMR

    Warmed and humidified where available. Ventilate at a normal or slightly reduced rate, since metabolic demand is low and hyperventilation can worsen arrhythmia risk.

    Assess for a long time before declaring absent vitals EMR

    Pulse and breathing may be extremely slow and shallow. Check carefully and for longer than usual before concluding they are absent, because starting compressions on a perfusing hypothermic heart can cause fibrillation.

    Cardiac arrest
    But with an extraordinary prognosis

    Hypothermic arrest is not like normal arrest. The cold that caused it also protects the brain, so resuscitation is continued far longer than usual and rewarming is part of the resuscitation. Nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.

    Prolong resuscitation EMR

    Standard rules do not apply. Continue CPR and rewarming; survival with intact neurology after prolonged hypothermic arrest is well documented, particularly in cold water drowning and avalanche burial.

    Stop and escalate
    • Shivering that has stopped in a cold patient
    • Reduced consciousness
    • Rigid muscles
    • Very slow or undetectable pulse and breathing
    • Any arrhythmia
    • Wet clothing, immersion, or prolonged exposure
    • A long lie in an older patient
    • Trauma with hypothermia, which is a far worse combination than either alone

    Telling it apart

    Not Death?

    The distinction that matters most. Fixed dilated pupils, rigidity, and undetectable vitals are all compatible with survivable severe hypothermia. Resuscitation is continued and rewarming attempted.

    Not Intoxication?

    Confusion, poor coordination, and slurred speech look identical, and alcohol causes hypothermia by preventing vasoconstriction. Assume both and measure a temperature.

    Not Stroke or hypoglycemia?

    Both cause confusion and can cause a patient to lie exposed for hours. Check a glucose and assess neurologically once warmed.
