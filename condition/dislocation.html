    Dislocation

    Bone ends are displaced out of the joint, where they can stretch or compress the nerves and vessels running alongside.

    In one breath

    The joint comes apart and the displaced bone sits where it should not. It is intensely painful, and the real urgency is that certain joints have major nerves and arteries running close enough that the displacement compromises them.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Force exceeds ligament strength
    Or the joint is inherently unstable

    The shoulder dislocates most easily because it trades stability for range of movement. The hip requires very high energy in a healthy adult, usually a dashboard impact, but dislocates readily if it has been replaced.

    Ask about previous dislocations EMR

    Recurrent shoulder dislocations often reduce very easily and the patient may know exactly what works. Prosthetic hips dislocate with minimal force and need to be handled with that in mind.

    Bone ends separate
    Locked in an abnormal position

    Surrounding muscles spasm around the displaced position and lock it there. This is why the limb is held rigidly and why any attempt to move it causes severe pain.

    Splint in the position found EMR

    Support it comfortably where it lies with padding and slings. Do not attempt reduction unless it is within your scope and the limb is pulseless with a long transport.

    Reduction PCP

    Requires analgesia and often sedation, and is generally done in hospital after imaging to exclude a fracture. Some services permit field reduction for a pulseless limb with a prolonged transport.

    The capsule and ligaments tear
    Often with a fracture

    The capsule tears to allow the displacement, and ligaments are stretched or ruptured. Fractures of the joint surfaces occur commonly alongside, which is why these are always imaged.

    Nerves and vessels are stretched or compressed
    The actual emergency

    Specific joints threaten specific structures with enough reliability to be worth knowing. Knee dislocation, meaning the tibiofemoral joint and not the kneecap, threatens the popliteal artery in a large proportion of cases. Posterior hip dislocation threatens the sciatic nerve. Shoulder dislocation threatens the axillary nerve, so check sensation over the deltoid.

    Check circulation, sensation, and movement first EMR

    Before anything else, and again after any movement, and document both with times. This finding determines the entire urgency of the case.

    Distal tissue loses perfusion
    Clock starts

    If an artery is compromised, distal tissue is ischemic from that moment. Muscle survives roughly four to six hours. A pulseless dislocated knee is a limb-threatening emergency, not an orthopaedic inconvenience.

    Oxygen EMR

    Only if hypoxic or shocked from associated injury.

    Treat a pulseless dislocation as time-critical EMR

    Particularly the knee. Rapid transport, pre-alert, and a clear statement that the limb is pulseless. Muscle dies within hours.

    The bone end can lose its own blood supply
    Hip and shoulder particularly

    The femoral head is supplied by vessels running in the joint capsule. Prolonged dislocation can cut that supply and cause the bone to die over subsequent months, which is why hip dislocations are reduced urgently rather than electively.

    Stop and escalate
    • Absent or reduced distal pulse
    • Any knee dislocation, because of the popliteal artery risk
    • Numbness or loss of movement distal to the joint
    • Hip dislocation, which is both high-energy and threatens the blood supply to the femoral head
    • Cold, pale, or mottled limb distally
    • Open dislocation
    • Prolonged time since the injury

    Telling it apart

    Not Fracture?

    Deformity at the joint with the limb locked in an abnormal position suggests dislocation. Deformity along a shaft suggests fracture. They coexist frequently and it is not a distinction to agonise over in the field.

    Not Patellar dislocation?

    The kneecap slips sideways, usually laterally, and often reduces spontaneously on straightening the leg. Completely different from a true knee dislocation, which is a limb-threatening injury.

    Not Fractured neck of femur?

    In an older patient after a fall, a shortened externally rotated leg is far more likely to be a hip fracture than a dislocation, unless the hip has been replaced.
