    Condition · Bronchioles

    Bronchiectasis

    Airways permanently widened and scarred by repeated infection, which then pool secretions and get infected again.

    In one breath

    Infection damages the airway wall, the airway stretches and loses its cilia, so it can no longer clear itself. Secretions pool in the widened section, bacteria colonise them, and the resulting infection damages the wall further. It is a self-perpetuating loop.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Something damages the airway wall
    Infection, obstruction, or cystic fibrosis

    Severe childhood pneumonia, whooping cough, tuberculosis, an inhaled foreign body causing chronic obstruction, cystic fibrosis, or an immune deficiency. In cystic fibrosis, abnormally thick secretions cause the same cycle from the start.

    anatomy → Bronchioles
    The airway wall is destroyed and stretches
    Permanently widened

    Inflammatory enzymes destroy the muscle and elastic tissue in the airway wall. Without them the airway dilates permanently, sometimes to several times its normal calibre. This is irreversible.

    The clearance mechanism is destroyed
    The escalator stops

    The ciliated cells that normally sweep mucus upward toward the pharynx are lost and replaced with scar and mucus-producing cells. The airway now produces more secretions and has no way to move them.

    Secretions pool in the widened airway
    Large volumes of daily sputum

    Because the dilated section is wider than the airways below and above it, and gravity does not favour drainage, secretions collect. These patients typically produce large volumes of sputum daily, often worst in the morning after lying flat overnight.

    Let them clear their own chest EMR

    These patients usually know exactly how to drain themselves and which positions work. Do not suppress the cough or rush them; it is their only clearance mechanism. Give them a bowl and time.

    Sit upright, and let them position themselves EMR

    Lying flat lets secretions redistribute across the lung. Many patients have a specific position they use for drainage and it is worth asking.

    Bacteria colonise the pooled secretions
    The loop closes

    Stagnant warm mucus is an excellent culture medium. Chronic colonisation, often with Pseudomonas, drives continuous inflammation which destroys more airway wall, which widens it further, which pools more secretions. This is the vicious cycle that defines the disease.

    Ask about their usual organisms and antibiotics EMR

    Many carry a rescue antibiotic pack and know what they are colonised with. That information is genuinely valuable to the receiving team.

    Airflow is obstructed
    Crackles, wheeze, breathlessness

    Mucus plugging and airway wall thickening obstruct flow. Coarse crackles are characteristic and often change or clear with coughing, which is a useful bedside distinction from the fine crackles of pulmonary edema.

    shared node → Ventilation and perfusion stop matching

    Salbutamol EMR

    Helps where there is a reversible bronchospastic component, which many of these patients have alongside the structural disease.

    Airway vessels can bleed
    Sometimes massively

    Chronic inflammation causes enlarged, fragile bronchial arteries to grow into the airway wall. These are systemic pressure vessels, not pulmonary ones, so when they rupture the bleeding can be torrential. Massive hemoptysis in bronchiectasis is a genuine airway emergency: patients drown rather than exsanguinate.

    Bleeding side down for hemoptysis EMR

    If you know which side is bleeding, lying on that side keeps blood from flooding the healthy lung. Suction ready, high-flow oxygen, and treat massive hemoptysis as the airway emergency it is.

    Acute infection tips the balance
    The usual reason you're called

    Increased sputum volume, change in colour, fever, and worsening breathlessness. Because there is no reserve, an infection that a normal person would shrug off produces respiratory failure here.

    shared node → Tissue oxygen falls

    Oxygen EMR

    Titrate to target, and consider that some of these patients retain carbon dioxide like COPD patients do.

    Stop and escalate
    • Massive hemoptysis, which threatens the airway rather than the circulation
    • Sudden increase in sputum volume with fever
    • Saturation below their usual baseline
    • Confusion or drowsiness
    • Exhaustion from prolonged coughing
    • Chest pain with sudden breathlessness, suggesting pneumothorax

    Telling it apart

    Not COPD?

    Bronchiectasis often starts younger, may have no smoking history, and produces much larger daily sputum volumes. Coarse crackles that clear with coughing favour bronchiectasis. The two frequently coexist.

    Not Pneumonia?

    Pneumonia is an acute event in previously normal lung. Bronchiectasis is a chronic condition with a longstanding history of daily sputum, and these patients get recurrent pneumonias on top of it.

    Not Pulmonary edema?

    Fine crackles that do not clear with coughing, frothy rather than purulent sputum, orthopnea, and a cardiac history.
