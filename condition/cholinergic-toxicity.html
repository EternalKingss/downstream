    Cholinergic toxicity

    Acetylcholine cannot be cleared, so every cholinergic synapse fires continuously and the patient drowns in their own secretions.

    In one breath

    Organophosphates and nerve agents disable the enzyme that switches off acetylcholine. Every parasympathetic effect runs continuously: secretions everywhere, tiny pupils, a slow heart, and eventually paralysis of the breathing muscles. It is also the one poisoning that can harm you.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    The patient is exposed
    Pesticides, nerve agents, some mushrooms

    Organophosphate and carbamate pesticides are the common cause, through agricultural exposure or deliberate ingestion. Nerve agents such as sarin are the deliberate-harm version. Absorption is through skin, inhalation, and ingestion, and skin absorption continues for as long as the chemical remains on them.

    Protect yourself and decontaminate EMR

    One of the very few poisonings that genuinely threatens responders, through skin contact and off-gassing. Full personal protective equipment, remove and bag all clothing, and irrigate the skin copiously with water. Decontamination comes before treatment, because otherwise you become the next patient.

    Consider it a multi-casualty scene EMR

    Agricultural incidents and deliberate releases produce multiple patients and contaminated responders. Get hazardous materials resources moving early.

    Acetylcholinesterase is disabled
    The off-switch is gone

    Acetylcholine is normally broken down within milliseconds of release by acetylcholinesterase. These agents bind and inactivate the enzyme, so acetylcholine accumulates at every synapse that uses it. With organophosphates the binding becomes permanent over hours, a process called ageing, which is why antidote timing matters.

    Parasympathetic effects run continuously
    Secretions everywhere

    Salivation, tearing, urination, defecation, gastrointestinal cramping, and vomiting. Plus bradycardia, pinpoint pupils, and bronchospasm with massive bronchial secretions. The classic mnemonics are SLUDGE and DUMBELS, but the underlying idea is simpler: every gland switched on and every smooth muscle constricted.

    Recognise the pattern EMR

    Pinpoint pupils, streaming secretions, bradycardia, and muscle twitching together is close to diagnostic. It is easily mistaken for opioid overdose because of the pinpoint pupils, but opioids dry secretions rather than causing them.

    The airway floods
    The actual cause of death

    Bronchial secretions and bronchoconstriction together are what kills these patients. Volumes can be extraordinary, described as drowning in secretions. Suction is not a supportive measure here, it is a primary intervention.

    shared node → Airway stops protecting itself

    Suction, relentlessly EMR

    The single most life-saving practical intervention available to you. Secretion volumes are enormous and continuous. Position for drainage and keep suctioning.

    Neuromuscular junctions overstimulate then fail
    Fasciculation, then paralysis

    At the neuromuscular junction, excess acetylcholine first causes fasciculation and weakness, then depolarising blockade and paralysis. The diaphragm is affected, so respiratory failure comes from muscle paralysis as well as from secretions.

    Ventilate expecting difficulty EMR

    Bronchoconstriction plus secretions makes these patients hard to ventilate. Expect high resistance and keep the airway clear between breaths.

    Central effects
    Confusion, seizures, coma

    Acetylcholine accumulation in the brain causes anxiety, confusion, seizures, and coma, and directly depresses the respiratory centre. So respiration is attacked at three levels at once: secretions, muscle paralysis, and central drive.

    Ventilation fails
    Three mechanisms together

    Flooded airways, paralysed respiratory muscles, and a suppressed brainstem. This is what kills, and it is why aggressive suction and ventilation are as important as the antidote.

    shared node → Breathing stops

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration with ventilation as needed, and expect to be suctioning constantly while you do it.

    Stop and escalate
    • Pinpoint pupils with copious secretions
    • Any suggestion of pesticide or chemical exposure
    • Multiple casualties with similar symptoms
    • Muscle fasciculation
    • Bradycardia with secretions
    • Seizures
    • Responders developing symptoms
    • A smell of solvent or garlic on the patient or their clothing

    Telling it apart

    Not Opioid overdose?

    Both give pinpoint pupils and respiratory failure. Opioids give dry secretions, a normal or slow heart rate without the gastrointestinal storm, and no fasciculation. Naloxone does nothing for cholinergic toxicity.

    Not Anticholinergic toxicity?

    The exact opposite: dry as a bone, hot, flushed, dilated pupils, and delirium. Caused by antihistamines, tricyclics, and some plants.

    Not Asthma or pulmonary edema?

    Both cause wheeze and secretions. The pinpoint pupils, the gastrointestinal symptoms, and the exposure history separate them.

    Not Treatment: why not epinephrine?

    Epinephrine is not a treatment for cholinergic toxicity and should not be given. The muscarinic effects are reversed by atropine. Pralidoxime reactivates acetylcholinesterase before the organophosphate bond ages and becomes permanent. Both are beyond EMR scope.
