    Condition · Adrenal glands

    Adrenal crisis

    Cortisol is missing, so the blood vessels stop responding to the body's own stress hormones.

    In one breath

    Cortisol is what lets catecholamines work on blood vessels. Without it a patient becomes hypotensive and cannot be brought back by the usual compensations, because the hormone that permits those compensations is absent. It looks like refractory shock and it is fixed by a steroid.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Cortisol production fails or is suppressed
    Steroid dependence is the usual cause

    By far the commonest cause is a patient on long-term steroids whose own adrenal function is suppressed, who then becomes ill or stops the steroid abruptly. Primary adrenal failure such as Addison's disease is much rarer, as is adrenal hemorrhage in overwhelming sepsis.

    anatomy → Adrenal glands

    Ask about steroids in every unexplained shock EMR

    The single intervention that matters. Long-term prednisone, hydrocortisone, or dexamethasone, or a history of Addison's disease. Many patients carry a steroid card, a medical alert bracelet, or an emergency hydrocortisone injection kit.

    Look for the patient's own emergency injection EMR

    Many known adrenal insufficiency patients carry an emergency hydrocortisone kit and have been trained to use it. Assisting a patient with their own prescribed emergency medication is often within scope, so ask, and check your service policy in advance rather than at the scene.

    A stress raises the requirement
    Infection, trauma, surgery, vomiting

    Cortisol output normally rises several-fold under physical stress. A patient who cannot mount that rise decompensates precisely when they most need the reserve. Vomiting is doubly dangerous because they also cannot absorb their oral steroid.

    Treat the trigger EMR

    Infection is the usual precipitant and often needs treating in its own right.

    Vessels stop responding to catecholamines
    Compensation fails

    Cortisol is permissive for vascular tone: without it, adrenaline and noradrenaline have far less effect on blood vessels. The patient's own sympathetic response, and any drug you give, both stop working properly. This is the mechanism behind the refractory hypotension.

    anatomy → Arteries and arterioles

    Hydrocortisone ACP

    The definitive treatment and it works within an hour or two. Outside EMR scope, so early recognition and a clear handover is your contribution.

    Sodium and water are lost
    Aldosterone deficiency too

    In primary adrenal failure aldosterone is also missing, so sodium and water are lost in the urine and potassium is retained. The result is hypovolemia with low sodium and high potassium, and the potassium itself can cause arrhythmias.

    anatomy → Body fluid and electrolytes
    Blood glucose falls
    Cortisol mobilises glucose

    Cortisol supports gluconeogenesis. Without it, glucose falls, adding confusion and weakness to the picture and sometimes causing seizures, especially in children.

    Shock that doesn't respond
    Fluid and pressors both fail

    Hypotension that does not improve with fluid or vasopressors is the signature. Recognising this pattern is what triggers the one treatment that works, which is steroid replacement.

    Supportive.

    Keep them horizontal and warm EMR

    Maximises venous return in a patient who cannot vasoconstrict effectively.

    Circulatory collapse
    Rapidly fatal if unrecognised

    Untreated adrenal crisis is fatal, and it kills patients who would recover completely with a drug that costs almost nothing. The whole problem is that it is not thought of.

    shared node → Circulation stops
    Stop and escalate
    • Hypotension that does not respond to fluid
    • Any history of long-term steroid use
    • Known Addison's disease or a steroid alert card
    • Vomiting in a steroid-dependent patient, since they cannot absorb their tablets
    • Hypoglycemia with hypotension
    • Abdominal pain, vomiting, and shock together
    • Hyperpigmented skin creases, which suggests Addison's disease

    Telling it apart

    Not Septic shock?

    Very similar, they frequently coexist, and infection is the usual trigger. The clue is shock that is disproportionate to the apparent infection and does not respond to fluid.

    Not Hypovolemic shock?

    Both are hypotensive with poor perfusion. Adrenal crisis does not respond to volume alone and there is usually no source of loss.

    Not Anaphylaxis?

    Both cause distributive shock. Anaphylaxis has skin and airway signs and follows an exposure.
