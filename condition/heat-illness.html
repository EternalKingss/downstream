    Heat exhaustion and heat stroke

    Heat gain outstrips the ability to lose it, and past a point the regulatory system itself fails.

    In one breath

    Heat exhaustion is a volume and salt problem with an intact brain. Heat stroke is a failure of thermoregulation itself, defined by altered mental state, and it cooks the patient's cells. The line between them is neurological, not thermal.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Heat gain exceeds heat loss
    Environment plus exertion

    Environmental heat, physical exertion generating internal heat, or both. Humidity is the critical modifier, because evaporation is the main cooling mechanism and it fails when the air is already saturated. Older adults, young children, and people on anticholinergics, antipsychotics, or diuretics are far more vulnerable.

    The body sheds heat
    Vasodilation and sweating

    Skin vessels dilate to bring blood to the surface and sweat glands produce fluid that removes heat as it evaporates. Both cost circulating volume: sweating loses water and salt, and vasodilation redistributes blood to the skin and away from the core.

    Fluid and salt are lost
    Heat exhaustion

    Heavy sweating without adequate replacement produces dehydration and salt depletion. Weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, and cramps, with heavy sweating and a normal or mildly raised temperature. Crucially, mental state is preserved: they are miserable but oriented.

    Oral fluids for heat exhaustion only EMR

    A fully alert heat exhaustion patient in a cool environment can be given oral fluid. Anyone with altered mental status gets nothing by mouth and needs IV fluid, which is beyond EMR scope.

    Thermoregulation itself breaks down
    Heat stroke begins

    As volume falls and the hypothalamic set point is overwhelmed, the cooling mechanisms fail. Sweating may cease entirely in classic heat stroke, though exertional heat stroke patients are often still drenched. Core temperature climbs above 40 degrees and continues rising.

    shared node → Core temperature rises

    Cold water immersion, or evaporative cooling EMR

    Immersion in cold water is the fastest method and is the standard in exertional heat stroke where facilities allow. Otherwise strip the patient, spray or sponge with tepid water, and fan continuously to drive evaporation. Ice packs to neck, axillae, and groin are an adjunct, not a substitute.

    Do not give antipyretics EMR

    Paracetamol and ibuprofen work by lowering the hypothalamic set point in fever. In heat stroke the set point is not the problem; the cooling machinery has failed. They do not work and they add liver and kidney injury to a patient who already has both.

    The brain is affected
    The defining feature

    Confusion, irritability, bizarre behaviour, ataxia, seizures, or coma. This is what separates heat stroke from heat exhaustion, and it is a neurological definition rather than a temperature one. Any altered mental state in a hot patient means heat stroke until proven otherwise.

    shared node → Level of consciousness drops

    Check glucose and look for other causes EMR

    Sepsis, thyroid storm, stimulant toxicity, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome all cause hyperthermia with altered mental state and are treated differently.

    Cells begin to cook
    Above about 40 to 41 degrees

    Cellular proteins denature and cell membranes fail. Damage is a function of both how high the temperature went and how long it stayed there, which is why speed of cooling directly determines outcome and why cooling takes priority over transport.

    Cool first, transport second EMR

    For heat stroke this reverses the usual priority. Outcome depends on how fast the core temperature comes down, and cooling en route is slower and less effective. Begin aggressive cooling on scene.

    Stop cooling around 38 to 39 degrees EMR

    Cooling has momentum and overshoot into hypothermia is a real risk. Monitor and stop before you reach normal.

    Muscle breaks down and organs fail
    Especially exertional heat stroke

    Muscle necrosis releases potassium and myoglobin. Liver injury, kidney failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation follow. Exertional heat stroke in young athletic patients is particularly associated with severe rhabdomyolysis.

    shared node → Damaged muscle releases its contents
    Cardiovascular collapse
    Distributive plus hypovolemic

    Massive vasodilation combined with genuine volume depletion produces profound shock. Mortality rises sharply with every minute the temperature stays elevated.

    shared node → Compensation fails

    Oxygen EMR

    Metabolic demand is enormously elevated. Give oxygen freely in heat stroke.

    Stop and escalate
    • Any altered mental state in a hot patient, which is heat stroke by definition
    • Core temperature above 40
    • Seizure
    • Hot dry skin, though its absence does not exclude heat stroke
    • Dark urine, suggesting rhabdomyolysis
    • Hypotension
    • A young athlete who collapsed during exertion
    • Older patients found in a hot dwelling during a heat wave

    Telling it apart

    Not Heat exhaustion?

    The distinction is neurological. Heat exhaustion patients are miserable, sweaty, weak, and fully oriented. Heat stroke patients have altered mental status. Get this right, because the urgency is completely different.

    Not Sepsis?

    Both give fever, tachycardia, and confusion. History and environment usually separate them, and they can coexist.

    Not Stimulant toxicity or serotonin syndrome?

    Cocaine, MDMA, and amphetamines cause hyperthermia directly and often with agitation. Serotonin syndrome adds rigidity, clonus, and hyperreflexia. Cooling is still the priority in all of them.

    Not Thyroid storm?

    Fever, tachyarrhythmia, agitation, and a history of thyroid disease or a recent precipitating illness.
