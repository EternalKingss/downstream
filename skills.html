    Skills

    Procedures rather than cascades, so they get a different format: every step carries the reason it exists.

    Knowing why a step is there is what lets you adapt when the real situation does not match the way you were taught it. It is also the difference between performing a skill and understanding one.

    Opening the airwayThe highest-value five seconds in prehospital medicine, and it costs nothing.Bag-valve-mask ventilationHarder than it looks, and the single most important skill for the most salvageable emergencies you will attend.Chest compressionsCompressions do not restart the heart. They keep the brain and the myocardium alive until something that can restart it arrives.DefibrillationThe only intervention that reverses ventricular fibrillation, and every minute of delay costs survival.External hemorrhage controlMechanical, sequential, and one of the few things you do that unambiguously saves lives.Spinal motion restrictionThe goal is preventing further movement of an unstable column, not strapping every patient to a board.Splinting and tractionSplinting is hemorrhage control and analgesia at the same time, not just tidiness.Recovery positionFree, instant, and it prevents the commonest avoidable death in unconscious patients.The primary surveyA deliberately ordered search for the things that kill fastest, in the order they kill.Oxygen delivery and titrationA drug with a dose, a target, and situations where more is worse.

    Downstream is a study tool, not a field reference and not medical direction. Doses and protocols change; your service’s medical control protocols are the authority of record. Scope tags reflect Alberta and should be verified against your own registration and service policy.
