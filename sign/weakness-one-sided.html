    One-sided weakness

    Usually a stroke, sometimes something that reverses completely, so check the reversible one first.

    What this sign actually means

    Motor pathways cross over, so weakness on one side means a problem on the opposite side of the brain, or a problem below the crossing in the cord or peripheral nerve. Because function is mapped to location, the pattern of deficit tells you roughly where the lesion sits.

    First moves
    • Check the glucose before anything else. Hypoglycemia produces identical focal deficits and reverses in minutes.
    • Establish the last known well time precisely, because it determines treatment eligibility.
    • Run a structured stroke screen and pre-alert.
    • Nothing by mouth, because swallowing is frequently impaired even in an alert patient.
    • Do not treat the blood pressure, which is usually maintaining flow to salvageable tissue.

    What produces it, and how to tell them apart

    Ordered roughly by how quickly each will kill. The clue column is what separates it from the others on this list.

    Ischemic stroke

    Sudden onset, face, arm, and speech involvement, atrial fibrillation or vascular risk factors.

    Hemorrhagic stroke

    Sudden severe headache, vomiting, rapid decline in consciousness, seizure at onset, very high blood pressure.

    Hypoglycemia

    Sweating, tremor, low glucose, and complete resolution with treatment. Always exclude it.

    Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state

    Older type 2 diabetic, very high glucose, profound dehydration. Focal signs occur and resolve with treatment.

    Seizure and status epilepticus

    Todd's paralysis after a witnessed seizure, resolving over minutes to hours. Still goes to a stroke centre.

    Traumatic brain injury

    Mechanism, external signs, deteriorating consciousness, unequal pupils.

    Spinal cord injury

    A level below which sensation and movement change, rather than one side of the body.

    Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

    Pregnancy or recent delivery with headache, visual changes, and hypertension.
