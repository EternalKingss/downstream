    Chest pain

    A handful of causes will kill within the hour and the rest will not, so the whole assessment is about separating them.

    What this sign actually means

    The chest contains the heart, great vessels, lungs, pleura, oesophagus, and a chest wall, and all of them generate pain. Visceral pain from the heart is dull, central, and poorly localised because it travels on autonomic fibres, which is why it refers to the jaw, arm, and back. Sharp pain that moves with breathing usually means pleura or chest wall.

    First moves
    • Assume cardiac until you have reason not to, and get pads on early in anyone high risk.
    • Rest them completely. No walking, no stairs, no self-transfer to the stretcher.
    • Saturation and oxygen only if below 94 percent.
    • Ask what they were doing at onset, whether it was maximal immediately, and what makes it better or worse.
    • Check blood pressure in both arms if dissection is a consideration.

    What produces it, and how to tell them apart

    Ordered roughly by how quickly each will kill. The clue column is what separates it from the others on this list.

    Acute coronary syndrome

    Pressure or heaviness, gradual build, radiation to jaw or arm, sweating, nausea, doom. May have no chest pain at all in women, older adults, and diabetics.

    Pulmonary embolism

    Sudden breathlessness dominating over pain, pleuritic and sharp, clear chest, risk factors.

    Ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm

    Tearing pain maximal at onset, radiating to back, pulsatile mass, hypotension. Aspirin is harmful here.

    Tension pneumothorax

    Unilateral absent breath sounds, progressive shortness of breath, shock, distended neck veins.

    Simple pneumothorax

    Sudden sharp pleuritic pain with unilateral reduced breath sounds and hyperresonance.

    Cardiac tamponade

    Penetrating chest trauma or a cardiac history, shock with distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds, equal breath sounds.

    Pneumonia

    Pleuritic pain with fever, cough, and focal crackles.

    Stimulant toxicity

    Chest pain after cocaine in a young person is a genuine infarct risk, not anxiety.

    Rib fractures

    Reproducible on palpation with a trauma history, and pain that stops them breathing deeply.

    Congestive heart failure

    Breathlessness dominating, orthopnea, crackles, swollen ankles.
