    Altered level of consciousness

    The most common presenting problem on this site and the least specific, because almost every cascade ends here.

    What this sign actually means

    The brain consumes a fifth of the body's oxygen while storing essentially no reserve of oxygen or glucose. Anything that interrupts either supply, poisons the cells, raises the pressure around them, or disrupts their electrical activity will change consciousness within minutes. That is why this sign appears at the end of so many chains and tells you almost nothing on its own about which chain you are in.

    First moves
    • Check glucose. Every time, without exception, before anything else diagnostic.
    • Check oxygen saturation and give oxygen if low. Restlessness and combativeness are hypoxia until proven otherwise.
    • Look at the pupils: pinpoint suggests opioids or a pontine lesion, unequal suggests raised intracranial pressure, dilated suggests stimulants or anticholinergics.
    • Position for airway protection. The reduced consciousness is now creating a second, mechanical problem on top of the first.
    • Take a temperature. Both hyperthermia and hypothermia cause this and both are treatable.

    What produces it, and how to tell them apart

    Ordered roughly by how quickly each will kill. The clue column is what separates it from the others on this list.

    Hypoglycemia

    Sweating, tremor, rapid onset, and it reverses completely with glucose. Check it first because it is the fastest thing to exclude.

    Opioid overdose

    Respiratory rate under 10, pinpoint pupils, and a history or paraphernalia. Ventilation fixes it before naloxone does.

    Traumatic brain injury

    Any mechanism, external signs, a deteriorating trend, or unequal pupils. Never let alcohol explain a falling GCS.

    Ischemic stroke

    Focal deficits with preserved alertness early. Establish a last known well time.

    Hemorrhagic stroke

    Sudden severe headache, vomiting, rapid decline, seizure at onset.

    Seizure and status epilepticus

    Witnessed convulsion, tongue biting, incontinence, and a postictal state that should be steadily improving.

    Diabetic ketoacidosis

    Deep sighing breathing, fruity breath, dehydration, high glucose.

    Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state

    Older type 2 diabetic, very high glucose, profound dehydration over a week, sometimes focal signs.

    Carbon monoxide poisoning

    Several people or pets from one building unwell together, normal-looking oximetry, winter, combustion appliance.

    Septic shock

    Fever or hypothermia with a source of infection. Confusion may be the only sign in an older adult.

    Meningitis and meningococcal sepsis

    Fever, headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, non-blanching rash.

    Alcohol intoxication and withdrawal

    A diagnosis of exclusion. Alcohol never explains deterioration, asymmetry, or a focal sign.

    Hypothermia

    Cold environment or a long lie. Shivering that has stopped is worse, not better.

    Heat exhaustion and heat stroke

    Hot environment or exertion. Altered mental state plus heat is heat stroke by definition.

    Hemorrhagic and hypovolemic shock

    Cerebral hypoperfusion from blood loss. Tachycardia, pale cool skin, and often no visible blood.
