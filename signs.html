    Start from a sign

    In the field you do not start with a diagnosis. You start with a patient who is confused, or breathless, or grey, and you work backwards.

    The rest of this site runs forward: anatomy, then what breaks it, then what happens next. These pages run the same chains in reverse. Pick what you can see, and every cascade that produces it is listed with the feature that separates it from the others.

    Altered level of consciousnessThe most common presenting problem on this site and the least specific, because almost every cascade ends here.Shortness of breathThe lung has five ways to fail, and the structure involved tells you which one you are in.Chest painA handful of causes will kill within the hour and the rest will not, so the whole assessment is about separating them.Shock and hypotensionFour mechanisms, and telling them apart determines whether fluid helps or harms.Abdominal painWhere it started and where it moved to is worth more than where it hurts now.One-sided weaknessUsually a stroke, sometimes something that reverses completely, so check the reversible one first.SeizureThe convulsion is rarely what harms them; the airway and the cause are.CyanosisA late sign that requires enough desaturated hemoglobin to be visible, so its absence proves nothing.Severe headacheMost are benign; the ones that are not announce themselves by how they started.Shock with no visible injuryIf they are shocked and there is nothing on the outside, the blood is on the inside.
