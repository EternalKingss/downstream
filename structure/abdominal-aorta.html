    Anatomy

    Abdominal aorta and great vessels

    The aorta descends through the abdomen just left of the midline and in front of the spine, dividing around the level of the umbilicus into the iliac arteries. The inferior vena cava runs alongside it, returning blood from everything below the diaphragm.

    What it does when it works

    • Carries the entire cardiac output destined for the abdomen, pelvis, and legs.
    • The wall has three layers, with a strong elastic middle layer bearing the pressure.
    • It lies retroperitoneally, behind the peritoneum and in front of the vertebral bodies.

    Why this structure matters

    Nothing else in the abdomen can kill this fast. A ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm can empty the circulation in minutes. Because it sits retroperitoneally, a leak may initially be contained behind the peritoneum, which produces a window of relative stability that ends abruptly when the containment fails.

    Why it matters in the field

    • Atherosclerosis weakens the wall over decades and it balloons. Most aneurysms are silent until they leak.
    • The classic triad is abdominal or back pain, hypotension, and a pulsatile abdominal mass, though all three together are uncommon.
    • Ruptured aneurysm is frequently mistaken for renal colic. An older patient with new severe flank or back pain and any hemodynamic instability needs this considered.
    • Do not palpate deeply or repeatedly if you suspect it.

    What can break it

    Failure mode: Catastrophic bleeding in seconds
