    Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

    A placental problem that presents as a whole-body vascular disease, and can end in seizures and stroke.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    The placenta implants abnormally
    Early in pregnancy

    Maternal spiral arteries fail to remodel properly, so the placenta is chronically underperfused. This happens in the first half of pregnancy, long before any symptom, which is why the disease cannot be prevented once established and why delivery is the only cure.

    anatomy → Uterus and placenta
    The placenta releases damaging factors
    Into the maternal circulation

    The stressed placenta releases anti-angiogenic factors and inflammatory mediators that damage vascular endothelium throughout the mother's body. This is the key concept: a placental problem becomes a systemic vascular disease.

    Blood vessel linings are damaged
    Everywhere at once

    Damaged endothelium causes vasoconstriction, capillary leak, and activation of clotting. Every organ is affected: kidneys leak protein, the liver swells and its capsule stretches causing right upper quadrant pain, platelets are consumed, and the brain becomes vulnerable.

    anatomy → Capillaries
    Blood pressure rises
    With protein in the urine

    The diagnostic combination after twenty weeks: hypertension with proteinuria, or hypertension with organ dysfunction. In a young pregnant patient whose baseline pressure is normally low, a pressure of 140/90 represents a substantial rise even though it would be unremarkable otherwise.

    Left lateral position EMR

    Improves venous return and uterine perfusion, and is also the safest position if she seizes.

    The brain is affected
    Headache, visual changes, hyperreflexia

    Cerebral vasospasm and oedema produce severe headache, visual disturbance such as flashing lights or blurring, and brisk reflexes. These are the warning signs immediately preceding a seizure and they must never be dismissed as normal pregnancy symptoms.

    Minimise stimulation EMR

    Light, noise, and movement can precipitate a seizure in a patient on the edge. Dim the compartment, keep it quiet, handle gently, and drive smoothly. This is a genuine clinical intervention rather than a courtesy.

    Take the warning symptoms seriously EMR

    Severe headache, visual disturbance, and upper abdominal pain in a pregnant patient are not minor complaints. They are the immediate warning signs of eclampsia and they change the urgency completely.

    Seizure occurs
    Eclampsia, by definition

    A generalised seizure in pre-eclampsia defines eclampsia. It can occur before, during, or up to several weeks after delivery, which catches people out. Any seizure in a pregnant or recently pregnant patient is eclampsia until proven otherwise.

    shared node → Neurons fire uncontrollably

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration during and after a seizure. Maternal hypoxia during a seizure directly compromises the fetus.

    Manage a seizure as usual, but recognise what it is EMR

    Protect from injury, recovery position on the left afterward, suction, oxygen, and check glucose. Communicate clearly that this is a pregnant patient, because the treatment is magnesium rather than a benzodiazepine.

    Magnesium sulphate ACP

    The specific treatment for eclamptic seizures and for preventing them, and it is superior to benzodiazepines here. Beyond EMR scope, so early recognition and pre-alert is what you contribute.

    Remember it happens after delivery too EMR

    A substantial proportion of eclamptic seizures occur postpartum, sometimes weeks later. A recently delivered patient with a seizure or a severe headache needs this considered.

    Liver and platelets fail
    HELLP syndrome

    Haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets. Presents with right upper quadrant or epigastric pain, nausea, and malaise, and is regularly mistaken for gastritis or gallbladder disease. It can occur with only mildly raised blood pressure.

    Stroke, abruption, or liver rupture
    The ways it kills

    Intracerebral hemorrhage is the leading cause of death, driven by severe hypertension in damaged vessels. Placental abruption, liver capsule rupture, pulmonary oedema, and renal failure also occur.

    shared node → Intracranial pressure rises

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    The placenta implants badly, becomes underperfused, and releases factors that damage blood vessel linings throughout the mother's body. Hypertension, protein loss, and capillary leak follow, and if it reaches the brain the patient seizes.

    Stop and escalate

    • Severe headache in pregnancy
    • Visual disturbance, flashing lights, or blurring
    • Right upper quadrant or epigastric pain
    • Any seizure in pregnancy or within six weeks of delivery
    • Blood pressure at or above 160 systolic
    • Rapidly increasing swelling of face and hands
    • Reduced fetal movements
    • Breathlessness, suggesting pulmonary oedema

    Telling it apart

    Not Epilepsy?

    A known epileptic can seize in pregnancy, but a first seizure in pregnancy is eclampsia until proven otherwise, and treating it as epilepsy delays the correct drug.

    Not Gastritis or gallbladder disease?

    HELLP syndrome presents with upper abdominal pain and nausea and is frequently misdiagnosed. Check the blood pressure in every pregnant patient with abdominal pain.

    Not Migraine?

    Migraine is common in pregnancy, but a new severe headache with visual changes and hypertension is pre-eclampsia until excluded.

    Not Stroke?

    Pre-eclampsia causes stroke, so they are not mutually exclusive. Focal deficits need urgent imaging either way.
