    Condition · Solid abdominal organs, Peritoneum

    Solid organ injury

    The liver, spleen, or kidney is torn, and bleeds into an abdomen that shows nothing on the outside.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Force is transmitted to the organ
    Compression, deceleration, or penetration

    Direct compression against the spine or ribs, deceleration tearing the organ away from its ligamentous attachments, or penetrating injury. The spleen is the most commonly injured in blunt trauma. Lower rib fractures on the left threaten the spleen, on the right the liver.

    anatomy → Solid abdominal organs
    The capsule and parenchyma tear
    Sometimes contained at first

    A subcapsular haematoma may form and be temporarily contained by an intact capsule. This is the mechanism behind delayed splenic rupture, where a patient is stable for hours or even days and then bleeds suddenly.

    Handle gently and move once EMR

    Rough handling can disrupt a contained subcapsular haematoma. Plan the move, do it once, and use a scoop rather than repeated log rolls.

    Blood pours into the abdomen
    No tamponade, no compression possible

    The peritoneal cavity holds litres before any visible distension, and there is nothing to apply pressure to. The liver receives about a quarter of cardiac output and the spleen holds a large reservoir, so both bleed heavily.

    shared node → Bleeding you cannot see

    Minimal scene time EMR

    No field intervention stops intra-abdominal bleeding. These patients need a surgeon, and time on scene is time bleeding.

    Nothing by mouth EMR

    Surgery is likely.

    Blood irritates the peritoneum
    Pain, guarding, shoulder tip pain

    Blood is an irritant. Guarding and tenderness develop, and blood under the diaphragm can refer pain to the shoulder tip, called Kehr's sign, because the diaphragm shares nerve roots with the shoulder. Left shoulder pain after blunt trauma is a splenic bleed until proven otherwise.

    shared node → The peritoneum becomes inflamed

    Document the abdomen serially EMR

    Distension, tenderness, and guarding compared over time is far more useful than a single assessment, and you may be the only person who saw the early picture.

    The body compensates
    Tachycardia with normal pressure

    A healthy young adult holds their blood pressure through substantial loss. The abdomen may be soft and only mildly tender. This is the stage where the diagnosis is made on mechanism and suspicion, not on findings.

    shared node → The body compensates

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration in significant trauma.

    Suspect it from mechanism, not findings EMR

    The abdomen is unreliable early. Significant blunt force, lower rib fractures, seat belt marks, handlebar injuries, or a fall from height all warrant treating this as present until excluded, regardless of how the abdomen feels.

    Clotting deteriorates
    Cold, acidotic, diluted

    Hypothermia slows clotting enzymes, acidosis from hypoperfusion impairs them further, and severe liver injury destroys the organ that manufactures clotting factors. The bleeding gets worse rather than better as time passes.

    anatomy → Blood

    Aggressive warming EMR

    One of the few things you can genuinely do about the bleeding. Hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy reinforce each other, and temperature is the part you control.

    Compensation collapses
    Sudden and late

    When it fails it fails quickly. This is why a stable-looking abdominal trauma patient is transported urgently rather than observed on scene.

    shared node → Compensation fails

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    These organs are blood-filled sponges wrapped in a fragile capsule. Tear one and it bleeds into a cavity that holds litres without looking distended. This is how trauma patients bleed to death with a completely unremarkable-looking abdomen.

    Stop and escalate

    • Shock with no external blood
    • Lower rib fractures
    • Seat belt bruising across the abdomen
    • Left shoulder tip pain after blunt trauma
    • Increasing abdominal distension
    • Tachycardia in a trauma patient with a normal blood pressure
    • Any hypotension in trauma
    • A child who looks well after a significant mechanism, since children compensate then crash

    Telling it apart

    Not Hollow organ injury?

    Hollow organ rupture produces peritonitis and rigidity over hours, driven by spilled contents rather than blood. Solid organ injury produces shock earlier with less dramatic abdominal findings.

    Not Pelvic fracture?

    Both bleed heavily and invisibly. Pelvic fracture has pelvic pain, leg length or rotation asymmetry, and sometimes blood at the urethral meatus, and it has a specific field treatment in the binder.

    Not Retroperitoneal bleeding?

    Kidney injury bleeds retroperitoneally rather than into the peritoneal cavity, so it causes flank pain and bruising with less peritoneal irritation, and often blood in the urine.
