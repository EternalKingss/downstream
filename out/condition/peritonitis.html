    Peritonitis and the acute abdomen

    Something has spilled inside the abdomen, and the membrane lining it is inflaming across a surface the size of the skin.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Something contaminates the peritoneum
    Perforation, infection, or ischemia

    A perforated ulcer or bowel spills acid and bacteria. Appendicitis or diverticulitis leaks pus. Pancreatitis releases digestive enzymes. Ischemic bowel dies and leaks. Ectopic pregnancy spills blood. All produce the same downstream picture.

    anatomy → Hollow abdominal organs
    Vague central pain first
    Autonomic innervation

    The visceral peritoneum covering the organs has autonomic innervation only, so pain from it is dull, crampy, poorly localised, and felt in the midline regardless of which organ is involved. This is why early appendicitis hurts around the umbilicus.

    anatomy → Peritoneum

    Take the history carefully EMR

    Where the pain started, where it is now, and how it changed. That migration is often the single most useful piece of information for the surgical team, and only you were there for it.

    Pain becomes sharp and localised
    Somatic innervation reached

    As inflammation reaches the parietal peritoneum lining the abdominal wall, which has somatic innervation, the pain becomes sharp, precisely localised, and dramatically worse with any movement that stretches the membrane. This migration is the classic evolution and it is diagnostically valuable.

    Drive gently EMR

    Not a courtesy. Jolting causes genuine severe pain in peritonitis, and pain on movement is itself a diagnostic feature worth documenting.

    Analgesia PCP

    The old teaching that analgesia masks the diagnosis has been abandoned. Adequate pain relief does not hide a surgical abdomen and withholding it is unjustified.

    The abdominal wall goes rigid
    Involuntary, and they stop moving

    Reflex muscle spasm splints the inflamed area. Involuntary guarding, rebound tenderness, and a patient lying completely still with knees drawn up who does not want to be moved or driven over speed bumps.

    shared node → The peritoneum becomes inflamed

    Position of comfort, usually knees flexed EMR

    Flexing the hips reduces tension on the abdominal wall and on the psoas. Do not force these patients flat.

    Litres of fluid weep into the abdomen
    The hidden shock

    The peritoneum has a surface area comparable to the skin. Inflamed, it exudes fluid across all of it, and inflamed bowel stops absorbing and starts secreting. Several litres can be sequestered out of the circulation with nothing lost externally. This is the most underestimated part of the condition.

    Treat them as hypovolemic EMR

    The key mental shift. This looks like a pain problem and is a volume problem. Horizontal, warm, and communicate the fluid requirement clearly on handover.

    Circulating volume falls
    Tachycardia, then hypotension

    Vomiting and reduced oral intake add to the third-space loss. These patients are genuinely hypovolemic and should be treated as shocked before the blood pressure ever falls.

    shared node → Tissue perfusion falls

    Oxygen EMR

    Titrate to target. Metabolic demand is raised by fever and inflammation.

    Bacteria enter the bloodstream
    Septic shock

    Faecal peritonitis in particular seeds enormous bacterial loads into the circulation. The combination of hypovolemia and distributive septic shock is why perforation carries such high mortality.

    shared node → Tissue perfusion falls

    Nothing by mouth EMR

    Surgery is likely, vomiting is common, and oral intake makes both worse.

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    Perforation, infection, or leaked digestive contents irritate the peritoneum. The pain localises and becomes unbearable on movement, and litres of fluid weep out of the circulation into the abdomen, so the patient goes into shock without losing a drop externally.

    Stop and escalate

    • Rigid, board-like abdomen
    • A patient who lies completely still and refuses to move
    • Tachycardia with a normal blood pressure, which is compensated shock
    • Fever with abdominal pain
    • Absent bowel sounds
    • Any abdominal pain in a woman of childbearing age, where ectopic pregnancy must be considered
    • Sudden onset severe pain, suggesting perforation
    • Confusion in an older patient with abdominal pain

    Telling it apart

    Not Ruptured aortic aneurysm?

    An aneurysm typically gives a soft abdomen with severe back or flank pain, because the blood is retroperitoneal. Peritonitis gives rigidity and guarding.

    Not Bowel obstruction?

    Obstruction gives colicky pain, distension, vomiting, and absent flatus, with a soft abdomen until the bowel perforates or becomes ischemic. Then it becomes peritonitis.

    Not Ectopic pregnancy?

    Must be considered in every woman of childbearing age with abdominal pain. Shoulder tip pain from blood irritating the diaphragm, with shock, is a rupture until proven otherwise.

    Not Diabetic ketoacidosis?

    DKA causes genuine abdominal pain and guarding and mimics a surgical abdomen convincingly. Check the glucose.

    Not Inferior myocardial infarction?

    Can present with epigastric pain, nausea, and vomiting. In an older patient with upper abdominal pain, this deserves consideration.
