    Hemothorax

    Blood fills the pleural space, collapsing the lung and draining the circulation at the same time.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    A vessel bleeds into the chest
    Intercostal, lung, or great vessel

    Rib fractures tearing intercostal vessels are a common cause, as is lung laceration. Injury to the great vessels or the heart produces a massive hemothorax that is usually rapidly fatal. A single hemithorax can hold well over a litre and a half of blood.

    anatomy → Pleural space
    Blood fills the pleural space
    Dull to percussion, collects at the base

    Unlike air, blood is heavy and pools in the dependent part of the chest. Breath sounds are reduced at the base and percussion is dull rather than hyperresonant, which is the bedside distinction from a pneumothorax.

    Do not seal a chest wound completely EMR

    A vented seal allows drainage and prevents tension. A fully occlusive dressing traps everything inside.

    Chest drainage ACP

    Definitive treatment is a chest tube, often followed by surgery. Outside EMR scope, so short scene time and a trauma centre destination is what you control.

    The lung is compressed
    Ventilation lost on that side

    The lung cannot expand into a space occupied by fluid. Functional lung volume falls, and the affected region is perfused but not ventilated.

    Blood passes unventilated lung
    Hypoxia

    As with any collapse, blood flows past lung that is not exchanging gas and returns unoxygenated.

    shared node → Ventilation and perfusion stop matching

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration. With reduced hemoglobin and reduced ventilated lung, maximising the saturation of what remains matters.

    Circulating volume is lost
    Into the chest, not onto the floor

    This is the part people underestimate. The blood is gone from the circulation just as surely as if it were on the ground, and it is invisible. The patient is bleeding heavily with no external evidence.

    anatomy → Blood

    Recognise it as bleeding EMR

    The most important mental shift. This is major hemorrhage that happens to be inside the chest. Treat the patient as a bleeding trauma patient, not simply a breathing problem.

    Hemorrhagic shock develops
    Flat neck veins, not distended

    Tachycardia, cool clammy skin, and eventually hypotension. Neck veins are typically flat because the patient is hypovolemic, which is an important distinction from tension pneumothorax and tamponade where they distend.

    shared node → Tissue perfusion falls

    Aggressive warming EMR

    Hypothermia impairs clotting, which worsens the bleeding into the chest. The lethal triad applies here as much as anywhere.

    Hypoxia and shock compound
    Two lethal problems, one injury

    The patient cannot oxygenate well and cannot deliver what oxygen they have. A massive hemothorax may also compress the mediastinum and impede venous return, adding an obstructive component.

    shared node → Compensation fails

    Minimal scene time EMR

    These patients need a surgeon and blood. Everything else is delay.

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    Two problems from one injury. The blood in the chest stops the lung expanding, and the blood in the chest is blood that is no longer in the circulation. A hemithorax can hold well over a litre, which is enough to kill on its own.

    Stop and escalate

    • Reduced breath sounds with dullness to percussion after chest trauma
    • Shock with flat neck veins after chest trauma
    • Rapidly worsening breathlessness with rising pulse
    • Any penetrating chest wound
    • Hypotension, which means substantial volume is already lost

    Telling it apart

    Not Tension pneumothorax?

    Tension gives hyperresonance and distended neck veins; hemothorax gives dullness and flat neck veins. Both give reduced breath sounds and shock.

    Not Cardiac tamponade?

    Tamponade has equal breath sounds, muffled heart sounds, and distended neck veins.

    Not Simple pneumothorax?

    A simple pneumothorax does not cause hemorrhagic shock. Shock with a chest injury means blood is going somewhere.
