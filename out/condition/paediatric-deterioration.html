    The deteriorating child

    Children compensate brilliantly and then decompensate suddenly, and they almost always get there through breathing rather than the heart.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Something stresses the child
    Usually respiratory or fluid loss

    Respiratory infection, bronchiolitis, croup, asthma, foreign body, or fluid loss through vomiting and diarrhoea. Primary cardiac problems are rare in children, which is the single most important structural difference from adult emergencies.

    Use a length-based tape for weights and doses EMR

    Estimating a child's weight by eye is unreliable and dosing errors in children are a well-documented harm. Use the tape.

    Their anatomy works against them
    Small airways, big heads, floppy chests

    Airways are proportionally much narrower, so a millimetre of swelling that an adult would not notice is catastrophic. The tongue is relatively large, the occiput is prominent so the neck flexes when supine, ribs are horizontal and cartilaginous so the chest wall contributes little, and they depend almost entirely on the diaphragm.

    anatomy → Chest wall and diaphragm

    Position the airway correctly for age EMR

    Infants need a neutral position, often with padding under the shoulders, because the large occiput flexes the neck and obstructs the airway when supine. Older children need progressively more extension. Over-extending an infant's neck closes the airway.

    Work of breathing rises visibly
    Retractions, flaring, grunting

    Because the chest wall is soft, effort is far more visible than in adults: intercostal, subcostal, and sternal retractions, nasal flaring, head bobbing, and grunting. Grunting is exhaling against a partly closed glottis to keep alveoli open, and it is an ominous sign of significant respiratory distress.

    Oxygen EMR

    Deliver in the least distressing way possible. A screaming child fighting a mask consumes more oxygen than the mask delivers. Blow-by held by a parent is often better than a perfectly fitted mask on a fighting child.

    Salbutamol EMR

    For genuine bronchospasm. Note that wheeze in an infant under one is usually bronchiolitis, where bronchodilators help little.

    Keep them calm and with their parent EMR

    Distress increases oxygen demand and work of breathing. Assess in the parent's arms, kneel to their level, and do the frightening things last. This is physiology, not bedside manner.

    They compensate hard
    Tachycardia and tachypnea, pressure held

    Children raise their heart rate and respiratory rate dramatically and vasoconstrict powerfully. Blood pressure is held normal until very late, so hypotension in a child is a pre-arrest sign rather than an early one. Capillary refill and mental state are far better indicators.

    shared node → The body compensates

    Keep them warm EMR

    Children have a large surface area relative to mass and lose heat fast, particularly infants. Hypothermia worsens everything and develops quickly even indoors.

    Behaviour changes before numbers do
    The best early warning

    A child who will not feed, will not play, does not respond to their parents normally, or is inconsolable is telling you something before any measurement will. Conversely a child who is interacting, playing, and interested in their surroundings is unlikely to be critically ill at that moment.

    Oral glucose EMR

    Children have small glycogen stores and become hypoglycemic quickly with illness or reduced intake. Check the glucose in every unwell child.

    Assess from the doorway first EMR

    Appearance, work of breathing, and circulation to the skin, judged before you touch them. A child's baseline behaviour and level of engagement is the most sensitive assessment tool available, and touching them first destroys it.

    Listen to the parents EMR

    A parent saying this is not like any illness they have had before is a genuinely reliable predictor of serious illness. Parental concern outperforms many formal scoring systems and should never be dismissed.

    They tire
    The quiet phase

    Small children have limited energy reserves and a diaphragm doing all the work. When effort decreases, retractions lessen, and the child becomes quiet or drowsy, they are not improving. This apparent settling is the most dangerous moment and it is regularly misread as recovery.

    shared node → Respiratory muscles fail
    Bradycardia from hypoxia
    Pre-arrest

    Unlike adults, children respond to profound hypoxia with bradycardia. A slow heart rate in a sick child is a terminal sign, and it is treated with oxygen and ventilation, not with cardiac drugs, because the cause is respiratory.

    Never delay ventilation EMR

    Because paediatric arrest is respiratory, effective ventilation is the treatment for almost everything, including bradycardia. A bradycardic child needs oxygen and breaths, immediately.

    Cardiac arrest
    Almost always hypoxic

    Paediatric arrest is usually the endpoint of respiratory failure or shock, so it is rarely shockable and outcomes are poor once reached. Everything in paediatric care is about intervening before this point, because that is where the wins are.

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    A child holds their vital signs normal far longer than an adult, then falls off a cliff. And the route to arrest is nearly always respiratory, which is why paediatric resuscitation is airway and breathing first, and why the signs to watch are effort and behaviour rather than blood pressure.

    Stop and escalate

    • Reduced work of breathing in a child who was working hard
    • Grunting
    • Bradycardia
    • Any hypotension, which is pre-arrest
    • Capillary refill over three seconds
    • Not feeding, or fewer wet nappies
    • Inconsolable, or abnormally quiet and unresponsive to parents
    • A parent who says this is different from any previous illness
    • Non-blanching rash
    • A history that does not match the injury, or a delay in seeking help

    Telling it apart

    Not Adult deterioration?

    Adults usually arrest from cardiac causes; children almost always from respiratory ones. That single difference drives the whole approach: airway and breathing first, and blood pressure is a late and unreliable guide.

    Not Bronchiolitis versus asthma?

    Under one year with a first episode of wheeze following a cold, it is usually bronchiolitis, and bronchodilators help little. Over two with recurrent episodes, asthma is more likely.

    Not Croup versus epiglottitis?

    Croup has a barking cough and stridor, usually settles, and is common. Epiglottitis is rare since vaccination but presents with drooling, a muffled voice, high fever, and a toxic-looking child sitting forward. Never put anything in the mouth or lie them flat if you suspect it.

    Not Non-accidental injury?

    Injuries inconsistent with the stated mechanism or the child's developmental stage, delayed presentation, changing history, or injuries in unusual sites. Document factually what you observed and follow safeguarding procedure. Children who are not yet mobile do not usually bruise themselves.
