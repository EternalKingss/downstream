    Stimulant toxicity

    Massive sympathetic overdrive that overheats the patient, strains the heart, and can kill through hyperthermia rather than the drug itself.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Catecholamines flood the system
    Released and not reabsorbed

    Stimulants block the reuptake of noradrenaline, dopamine, and serotonin, and amphetamines also force their release. The result is sustained sympathetic activation far beyond anything the body produces naturally, acting on every adrenergic receptor at once.

    anatomy → Autonomic nervous system

    Benzodiazepines PCP

    The mainstay of treatment: they reduce agitation, lower heart rate and blood pressure, drop temperature, and treat seizures, all by damping the central drive. Outside EMR scope, so early escalation matters.

    Heart rate, contractility, and pressure surge
    Beta-1 and alpha-1

    Beta-1 stimulation raises rate and force of contraction, sharply increasing myocardial oxygen demand. Alpha-1 stimulation constricts arteries and raises blood pressure, increasing afterload. Both push demand up together.

    anatomy → Myocardium
    Coronary arteries constrict
    Demand up, supply down

    The specific lethality of cocaine. It constricts the coronary arteries at the same moment it raises myocardial demand, so supply and demand move in opposite directions. It also promotes platelet aggregation and accelerates atherosclerosis. Cocaine causes infarcts in people in their twenties with clean arteries.

    anatomy → Coronary arteries

    Treat chest pain as cardiac EMR

    Chest pain after cocaine use in a young person is a genuine infarct risk and must not be dismissed on the basis of age. Note that beta blockers are avoided in cocaine toxicity because blocking beta receptors leaves alpha-mediated vasoconstriction unopposed.

    The myocardium starves
    Chest pain, arrhythmia

    Ischemia in a hyperadrenergic heart is intensely arrhythmogenic. Ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation, aortic dissection, and infarction all occur, and the risk persists for hours after use.

    shared node → Heart muscle starves

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration. Demand is enormously elevated and delivery may be impaired by coronary constriction.

    Temperature climbs
    The main killer

    Heat production rises from agitation and muscular activity while vasoconstriction impairs heat loss through the skin. In MDMA toxicity this is compounded by dancing in hot environments. Temperatures above 41 degrees denature proteins and are the commonest mechanism of death in stimulant toxicity.

    shared node → Core temperature rises

    Cool them aggressively EMR

    The highest-value intervention. Remove clothing, wet the skin, fan, apply ice to the neck, axillae, and groin. Hyperthermia is what kills, and cooling is what prevents it. Treat a hot agitated patient as a thermal emergency.

    Check glucose and temperature EMR

    Measure a temperature rather than estimating it. Hyperthermia is frequently missed because nobody takes it in an agitated patient, and it is the finding that changes the whole management.

    Severe agitation and psychosis
    And the restraint risk

    Paranoia, hallucinations, and violent agitation. This is where restraint enters, and where sustained struggling against restraint produces a lactic acidosis that the patient cannot compensate for if their chest is restricted. Seizures occur directly from the drug as well.

    shared node → Neurons fire uncontrollably

    Never restrain prone EMR

    A patient generating severe lactic acidosis needs an enormous minute volume to compensate. Prone restraint or weight on the chest removes exactly that capacity at exactly the wrong moment. Supine or lateral, chest completely free, monitored continuously.

    De-escalate and reduce stimulation EMR

    Quiet, dim, few people, calm voices. Physical struggle directly worsens the acidosis and the hyperthermia, so genuinely calming the patient is a physiological intervention with measurable benefit.

    Muscle breaks down
    Hyperthermia plus exertion

    Sustained maximal muscular activity plus hyperthermia destroys muscle, releasing potassium and myoglobin. Hyperkalemia causes arrhythmia and myoglobin causes renal failure.

    shared node → Damaged muscle releases its contents
    Cardiovascular collapse
    Often just as they go quiet

    Severe acidosis, hyperthermia, hyperkalemia, and catecholamine excess combine. Collapse frequently occurs at the moment the patient stops struggling, which is why sudden calm during a violent restraint is an emergency and not a resolution.

    shared node → Circulation stops

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    Cocaine, methamphetamine, and MDMA flood the body with catecholamines and stop them being cleared. Everything speeds up and heats up. The heart is squeezed between rising demand and constricted coronary arteries, and the temperature climbs until proteins start denaturing.

    Stop and escalate

    • Temperature above 39
    • Chest pain at any age
    • Sudden calm during a struggle
    • Any prone restraint
    • Seizure
    • Dark urine
    • Altered consciousness
    • Severe hypertension with headache or neurological signs

    Telling it apart

    Not Heat stroke?

    Overlapping physiology and management, and stimulants are a common cause of hyperthermia. Cooling is the priority in both.

    Not Serotonin syndrome?

    Adds rigidity, clonus especially at the ankles, and hyperreflexia. Often follows a combination of serotonergic drugs rather than a single stimulant.

    Not Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal?

    Also produces agitation, tremor, tachycardia, sweating, and seizures. History and pupils help: stimulants dilate pupils markedly, and withdrawal follows cessation rather than use.

    Not Thyroid storm or sepsis?

    Both cause fever, tachycardia, and agitation. Check glucose, look for a source, and treat the hyperthermia regardless of cause.
