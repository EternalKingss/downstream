    Thermal burns

    The barrier is destroyed, so fluid pours out and organisms get in, and the damage keeps extending after the heat is gone.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Heat destroys tissue
    Temperature times time

    Protein denatures above roughly 44 degrees, and the damage depends on both temperature and duration of contact. Scalds from hot liquid, contact with hot surfaces, flame, and steam all differ in the depth pattern they produce. Children and older adults burn deeper at lower temperatures because their skin is thinner.

    Stop the burning process and remove constrictions EMR

    Remove smouldering clothing and anything hot, but leave anything stuck to the skin. Remove rings, watches, and bracelets immediately, before swelling makes them tourniquets.

    Three zones form around the injury
    The middle one is salvageable

    A central zone of coagulation is dead. Around it is a zone of stasis where tissue is alive but poorly perfused and can go either way. Outside that is a zone of hyperaemia which recovers. The zone of stasis is what prehospital care influences: hypoperfusion, hypothermia, and further heat all convert it into dead tissue, which is how burns deepen after the event.

    Cool the burn, warm the patient EMR

    Cool running water for around twenty minutes, ideally within the first three hours, which genuinely reduces burn depth by protecting the zone of stasis. Then stop, dry the surrounding area, and actively warm the patient. Never use ice, and never leave a large burn under continuous cooling.

    Depth determines whether it can regenerate
    And whether it hurts

    Superficial burns involve epidermis only, are red and painful and blanch. Partial thickness reaches the dermis, blisters, weeps, and is extremely painful because nerve endings are exposed. Full thickness destroys the dermis entirely, so it is dry, leathery, white or charred, and painless. A painless burn is worse than a painful one, which is the opposite of intuition.

    The barrier fails
    Fluid out, organisms in

    The dead outer layer of skin is what keeps water in and bacteria out. Without it, evaporative loss increases enormously and the burn surface becomes an ideal culture medium. Infection is the leading cause of death in patients who survive the initial injury.

    Cover with clean dry non-adherent dressing EMR

    Cling film laid on lengthwise, not wrapped circumferentially, is ideal: it does not stick, reduces pain by covering exposed nerve endings, and lets the burn team see the wound. Do not apply creams, ointments, or home remedies.

    Watch the airway in any facial or inhalation burn EMR

    Soot, hoarseness, singed nasal hair, or facial burns mean the airway will swell over hours. Escalate before it becomes a problem, not after.

    Plasma leaks out of the circulation
    Over hours, not minutes

    Heat and inflammatory mediators make capillaries leaky, locally in small burns and body-wide in large ones. Plasma moves into the interstitium and out through the surface. Losses peak in the first eight hours and continue for a day or more.

    Estimate the burn area properly EMR

    Rule of nines in adults, adjusted for children whose heads are proportionally larger. The patient's own palm including fingers is roughly one percent, which is useful for scattered burns. Count partial and full thickness only, never superficial redness.

    Hypovolemic shock develops
    Delayed by hours

    The critical timing point. Burn shock is not immediate. A large burn patient who is hemodynamically normal at the roadside is early, not stable. Conversely, a burn patient who is shocked within minutes has something else wrong: bleeding, a tension pneumothorax, or another injury.

    Oxygen EMR

    High concentration for any significant burn, and mandatory for anything involving fire in an enclosed space, where carbon monoxide must be assumed regardless of the oximeter reading.

    Anticipate the shock rather than react to it EMR

    Large burns need substantial fluid resuscitation started early, before the shock appears. Getting an accurate area estimate and time of injury to the receiving team drives that calculation.

    They lose heat rapidly
    And cooling makes it worse

    Burned skin cannot regulate temperature and evaporative loss is enormous. Over-enthusiastic cooling of a large burn causes dangerous hypothermia, which worsens perfusion of the zone of stasis and impairs clotting. This is a genuine and common iatrogenic harm.

    Circumferential burns act like a tourniquet
    Limb or chest

    Full-thickness burn forms a rigid inelastic layer. As the tissue beneath swells, that layer constricts like a tourniquet. Around a limb it causes compartment syndrome; around the chest it physically prevents the chest wall expanding, causing ventilatory failure with healthy lungs.

    Escharotomy ACP

    Surgically incising a constricting circumferential burn. Outside prehospital scope for most services, so recognising a chest that cannot expand or a limb losing perfusion, and communicating it urgently, is your role.

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    A burn is not a fixed injury. Tissue at the margins is stunned rather than dead and can be saved or lost depending on what happens in the next hours. Meanwhile plasma leaks out through and around the burn for a day, which is why burn shock arrives late rather than immediately.

    Stop and escalate

    • Any facial burn, soot around the mouth or nose, or hoarseness
    • Enclosed space fire
    • Circumferential burns of a limb or the chest
    • Burns over 10 percent in a child or 15 percent in an adult
    • Burns to hands, feet, face, genitals, or across joints
    • Full thickness burns of any size
    • Shock in the first minutes, which means another injury
    • Any burn in a child where the history does not fit the pattern

    Telling it apart

    Not Chemical burns?

    Chemicals continue damaging until removed, so irrigation is prolonged rather than twenty minutes. Alkalis penetrate deeper than acids and need longer.

    Not Electrical burns?

    Surface appearance massively understates the damage, because current travels through deep tissue. Small entry and exit wounds can conceal extensive muscle destruction.

    Not Non-accidental injury?

    Sharply demarcated immersion lines, glove or stocking distribution, symmetrical burns, burns to buttocks or perineum, or a history that changes or does not match the injury. Report your observations factually and follow safeguarding procedure.
