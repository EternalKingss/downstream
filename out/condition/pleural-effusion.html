    Pleural effusion

    Fluid accumulates slowly in the pleural space, compressing the lung from outside.

    The chain

    Each step causes the one below it. Interventions are attached to the link they break, and greyed where they sit outside EMR scope in Alberta.

    Fluid production outpaces drainage
    Pressure, protein, or inflammation

    The pleural space normally holds a few millilitres, continuously produced and reabsorbed. Three things upset that: raised hydrostatic pressure from heart failure, low plasma protein from liver or kidney disease reducing the oncotic pull, or inflammation and malignancy increasing capillary leak and blocking lymphatic drainage.

    anatomy → Pleural space

    Look for the underlying cause EMR

    Heart failure, liver disease, kidney failure, cancer, pneumonia, or a pulmonary embolism. The effusion is a symptom, and identifying the cause changes everything the hospital does next.

    Fluid accumulates slowly
    Weeks, not minutes

    This is the critical difference from a hemothorax. Slow accumulation lets the pleural space and the chest wall gradually accommodate, so a patient can hold well over a litre with surprisingly modest symptoms. Rate matters far more than volume.

    The lung is compressed from below
    Dull to percussion, absent breath sounds

    Fluid is heavy and collects in the dependent part of the chest, compressing the lower lobe upward. Breath sounds are reduced or absent at the base with dullness to percussion, and often bronchial breathing just above the fluid level where compressed lung transmits sound.

    Drainage ACP

    Definitive treatment is drainage in hospital. Outside prehospital scope, and rarely urgent unless the effusion is massive.

    Functional lung volume falls
    Breathless on exertion first

    Less lung is available to ventilate and the chest is mechanically restricted. Symptoms typically appear on exertion long before they appear at rest, which is why these patients present late.

    shared node → Work of breathing rises

    Sit upright EMR

    Lets the fluid settle to the base and gives the remaining lung the best chance to expand. Many of these patients cannot lie flat at all.

    Compressed lung is perfused but not ventilated
    Mild hypoxia

    Blood flows past compressed lung and returns unoxygenated. Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction reduces this over time, which is why hypoxia is often milder than the size of the effusion suggests.

    shared node → Ventilation and perfusion stop matching

    Oxygen EMR

    Titrate to target. The response is usually good, because this is compression rather than a true fixed shunt.

    A very large effusion shifts the mediastinum
    Rare, but it happens

    A massive effusion can push the mediastinum toward the other side and impede venous return, producing a picture resembling a tension pneumothorax. This is uncommon but is a genuine emergency when it occurs.

    Treat sudden change as something else EMR

    Effusions build over weeks. A patient with a known effusion who deteriorates suddenly has developed something new, most likely a pneumothorax, an infection, or a pulmonary embolism.

    Test yourself

    In one breath

    The same mechanical problem as a hemothorax, arriving over weeks instead of minutes. Because it is slow, the pleural space stretches to accommodate it, and a patient can carry a litre or more while still walking around.

    Stop and escalate

    • Sudden deterioration in a known effusion, which means a new problem
    • Fever, suggesting infected fluid
    • Hypotension with distended neck veins, suggesting a massive effusion compressing the mediastinum
    • Inability to lie flat at all
    • Chest pain with sudden breathlessness

    Telling it apart

    Not Hemothorax?

    Identical mechanics, entirely different timescale and urgency. Hemothorax follows trauma, develops in minutes, and causes hemorrhagic shock. Effusion develops over weeks with no bleeding.

    Not Pneumothorax?

    Air is hyperresonant to percussion and collects at the apex. Fluid is dull and collects at the base.

    Not Consolidation from pneumonia?

    Both give dullness and reduced breath sounds. Pneumonia has fever, crackles, and bronchial breathing throughout rather than only at the upper margin.
