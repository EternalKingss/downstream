    Respiratory muscles fail

    The muscles that were compensating run out of fuel.

    What is actually happening

    The diaphragm and accessory muscles are skeletal muscle, and they fatigue like any other. When a patient has been working many times harder than normal for a sustained period, and is simultaneously hypoxic, those muscles begin to fail.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It is the endpoint of prolonged increased work of breathing, made worse because the muscles doing the work are themselves being starved of oxygen.

    What you see

    • A patient who was working hard and now appears calmer or sleepier without any treatment
    • Falling respiratory rate in a patient who was previously tachypneic
    • Decreasing chest excursion and quieter breath sounds
    • In asthma, a silent chest, because there is no longer enough air movement to generate a wheeze

    Test yourself

    The trap

    This looks like improvement and it is the opposite of improvement. A quieting asthmatic with a falling respiratory rate is minutes from arrest, not recovering. Any apparent improvement without a corresponding intervention should terrify you.
