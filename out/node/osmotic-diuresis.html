    Sugar drags water out of the body

    Glucose spills into the urine and pulls litres of water and electrolytes with it.

    What is actually happening

    The kidney reabsorbs all filtered glucose until blood levels exceed roughly 10 mmol/L. Above that the transporters are saturated and glucose remains in the urine, where it is osmotically active and holds water in the tubule. The patient produces enormous volumes of urine and loses sodium, potassium, and magnesium along with it.

    Why it follows from almost anything

    It follows any sustained hyperglycemia, and it is the mechanism behind the dehydration in both diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar states.

    What you see

    • Passing large volumes of urine frequently, often the first symptom the patient noticed
    • Extreme thirst and a dry mouth
    • Dry mucous membranes, sunken eyes, poor skin turgor
    • Tachycardia and postural dizziness
    • Weight loss over days

    Test yourself

    The trap

    The fluid deficit is far larger than it looks. A patient in established ketoacidosis is typically five to eight litres down, and a hyperosmolar patient can be ten or more. They arrived at that deficit over days, so they compensate remarkably well until they suddenly do not. Treat them as profoundly dehydrated regardless of how stable the blood pressure looks.
