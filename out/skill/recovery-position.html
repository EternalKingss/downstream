    Recovery position

    Free, instant, and it prevents the commonest avoidable death in unconscious patients.

    EMR

    Why it works

    An unconscious patient loses the tone that holds the tongue forward and loses the reflexes that clear secretions. Lateral positioning uses gravity for both: the tongue falls forward rather than back, and fluid drains out of the mouth rather than into the trachea.

    How to do it, and why each step exists

    Every step below has a reason. If you know the reason, you can adapt when the situation does not match the textbook.

    Use it for any unconscious patient who is breathing adequately

    Overdose, post-ictal, intoxication, post-arrest with return of circulation, or hypoglycemia awaiting recovery. If they are breathing and not protecting their airway, they belong on their side.

    Keep the head slightly extended and the mouth lowest

    Allows drainage and keeps the airway aligned. A head that flops forward onto the chest closes the airway even in the lateral position.

    Support the position so they stay there

    Patients roll back, especially in a moving vehicle. Use the upper leg flexed and pillows or a supporting hand.

    Have suction ready anyway

    Position helps drainage but does not remove everything, and vomiting can outpace gravity.

    Reassess breathing continuously

    The reason they are unconscious may still be progressing. Position solves the airway, not the cause.

    Modify for suspected spinal injury

    Use a coordinated log roll with in-line stabilisation, or a lateral position on a scoop. A patent airway always takes priority over spinal precaution.

    Common errors

    • Leaving an unconscious patient supine because they seem to be breathing fine
    • Not securing the position so the patient rolls back
    • Head flexed forward, which closes the airway
    • Assuming positioning replaces the need for suction
    • Using it in a patient who is not breathing adequately, who needs ventilation instead

    Where you use it

    Opioid overdoseSeizure and status epilepticusAlcohol intoxication and withdrawalGastrointestinal bleeding
