    Reverse lookup

    Severe headache

    Most are benign; the ones that are not announce themselves by how they started.

    What produces it, and how to tell them apart

    Ordered roughly by how quickly each will kill. The clue is what separates it from the others on this list.

    Hemorrhagic stroke

    Instantly maximal, worst ever, vomiting, neck stiffness, sometimes collapse at onset.

    Meningitis and meningococcal sepsis

    Fever, neck stiffness, photophobia, non-blanching rash, developing over hours.

    Traumatic brain injury

    Mechanism, external signs, deteriorating consciousness, and worse if anticoagulated.

    Carbon monoxide poisoning

    Multiple people affected, improves away from the building, normal-looking oximetry.

    Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

    Pregnant or recently delivered, hypertension, visual disturbance, epigastric pain.

    Hypertension related

    Very high blood pressure with neurological signs. Note that most high readings with headache are not emergencies; the neurology is what matters.

    Heat exhaustion and heat stroke

    Hot environment, exertion, with weakness and nausea.

    Test yourself

    First moves

    • Ask whether it was instantly maximal. A thunderclap onset is a subarachnoid hemorrhage until proven otherwise.
    • Take a temperature and look for neck stiffness and rash.
    • Full neurological assessment including pupils and limb power.
    • Check the blood pressure, and in a pregnant patient treat hypertension with headache as pre-eclampsia.
    • Dim the lights and minimise stimulation, which is both humane and clinically useful.

    What this sign actually means

    Brain tissue itself has no pain receptors. Headache comes from the meninges, blood vessels, and surrounding structures. That means a headache signalling something dangerous is usually signalling stretched or irritated meninges, distended vessels, or rising pressure.
