    Shortness of breath

    The lung has five ways to fail, and the structure involved tells you which one you are in.

    What produces it, and how to tell them apart

    Ordered roughly by how quickly each will kill. The clue is what separates it from the others on this list.

    Asthma

    Expiratory wheeze, prolonged expiration, known trigger, previous similar attacks that resolved fully.

    COPD

    Smoking history, chronic productive cough, barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, a baseline they never return from.

    Pulmonary edema

    Orthopnea, frothy or pink sputum, basal crackles, ankle swelling, cardiac history.

    Pneumonia

    Fever, focal crackles, purulent sputum, pleuritic pain.

    Pulmonary embolism

    Sudden onset, clear chest, hypoxia out of proportion to the exam, risk factors, unilateral leg swelling.

    Tension pneumothorax

    Unilateral absent breath sounds with shock and distended neck veins. Progressive deterioration.

    Simple pneumothorax

    Unilateral absent breath sounds, hyperresonance, sudden pleuritic pain, tall thin young patient or COPD.

    Hemothorax

    Unilateral absent breath sounds with dullness, after trauma, with shock and flat neck veins.

    Anaphylaxis

    Exposure, urticaria, angioedema, voice change or stridor, hypotension. More than just the airway.

    Congestive heart failure

    Orthopnea, ankle swelling, waking breathless at night, known cardiac history, a clear trigger such as infection or missed medication.

    Hemorrhagic and hypovolemic shock

    Breathlessness without lung findings, because the problem is delivery rather than gas exchange.

    Diabetic ketoacidosis

    Deep, rapid, sighing breathing that is compensation for acidosis rather than distress. Do not suppress it.

    Neuromuscular respiratory failure

    Clear lungs, weak cough, cannot count far on one breath, alert and frightened. Saturation is a trap here.

    Carbon monoxide poisoning

    Normal-looking saturation with breathlessness and headache, often several people affected.

    Test yourself

    First moves

    • Sit them upright unless they are hypotensive.
    • Saturation, and give oxygen to target.
    • Listen: wheeze, crackles, or a clear chest. A clear chest with severe hypoxia is a specific and important finding.
    • Compare the two sides. Unilateral absent breath sounds changes everything immediately.
    • Count the respiratory rate properly and note the effort, and remember that falling effort is deterioration.

    What this sign actually means

    Breathlessness means the body cannot meet its gas exchange demand. That happens if air cannot flow, if gas cannot cross the membrane, if the lung cannot expand, if blood is not arriving to collect the oxygen, or if nothing is driving the pump. It also occurs with no lung problem at all, when the heart cannot move blood or the blood cannot carry oxygen.
