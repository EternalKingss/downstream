    Medication · Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA)

    Salbutamol (Albuterol, Ventolin)

    Relaxes bronchial smooth muscle. It reverses spasm and nothing else.

    EMR  Inhaled medications including salbutamol fall within Alberta EMR scope. Whether you carry it or assist the patient with their own depends on your service.

    Mechanism

    Salbutamol binds beta-2 adrenergic receptors on bronchial smooth muscle. That activates adenylyl cyclase, which raises intracellular cyclic AMP, which reduces available intracellular calcium. Smooth muscle needs calcium to contract, so with less of it the muscle relaxes and the airway widens. It also has a modest mast cell stabilising effect that slows further mediator release.

    Why it works at that link and not another

    Remember that an asthmatic airway narrows three ways at once: spasm, swelling, and mucus. Salbutamol reverses exactly one of those three. This is not a limitation to memorise, it is the explanation for the most confusing thing you will see in the field. A severe asthmatic can receive a full dose and improve only slightly, because two thirds of their obstruction is untouched by the drug.

    Dosing

    RouteDoseNotes
    Metered dose inhaler with spacer4 to 8 puffs of 100 mcgRepeat every 20 minutes as needed. A spacer substantially improves delivery; without one, most of the dose lands in the mouth.
    Nebulised, adult2.5 to 5 mg in 3 mL salineDriven by oxygen at 6 to 8 L/min. Repeat every 20 minutes, or continuously in severe cases under direction.
    Nebulised, paediatric2.5 mg under 20 kg, 5 mg over 20 kgThe dose is not strictly weight-based, because what reaches the airway depends more on tidal volume than on body mass.

    Target: Titrate to effect. Improved air entry, reduced work of breathing, longer sentences, and improving saturation.

    Timing

    This is the part that tells you when to reassess, and it is the part most people never learn properly.

    Onset
    5 to 15 minutes.
    Peak
    30 to 60 minutes.
    Duration
    3 to 6 hours.

    What working looks like

    • Air entry improves and the chest sounds louder, which can mean the wheeze gets worse before it gets better as air reaches previously silent regions
    • Expiratory phase shortens
    • The patient starts speaking in longer phrases
    • Accessory muscle use decreases
    • SpO2 improves last, well behind the clinical picture

    What it means if nothing happens

    If there is no improvement after repeated doses, the obstruction is mostly swelling and mucus rather than spasm, or the diagnosis is wrong. Reconsider anaphylaxis, pulmonary edema, foreign body, and pneumothorax. A patient who is not responding is a patient who is heading toward the fatigue end of the cascade.

    Side effects are the drug doing its job elsewhere

    Every common side effect is the same drug action happening somewhere you did not want it. Tremor is beta-2 receptors on skeletal muscle. Tachycardia and palpitations are beta-1 spillover in the heart. Anxiety and restlessness are central sympathetic effects, and are genuinely difficult to tell apart from worsening hypoxia. Hypokalemia happens because beta-2 stimulation drives potassium into cells. None of these are mysterious. They are all beta agonism, just in the wrong tissue.

    Cautions

    Existing tachydysrhythmia or significant cardiac disease

    Beta-2 selectivity is good but not absolute. At higher doses salbutamol spills onto beta-1 receptors in the heart and increases rate and contractility.

    Wheeze that is not asthma

    Cardiac wheeze from pulmonary edema can sound identical. Salbutamol will not help and the tachycardia it causes increases myocardial oxygen demand in a patient who cannot afford it.
