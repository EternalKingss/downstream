    Medication · Nitrate vasodilator

    Nitroglycerin (GTN, glyceryl trinitrate)

    Dilates veins so less blood returns to the heart, which cuts the work the heart has to do.

    scope varies  Scope varies. Some Alberta first-response protocols place nitroglycerin at EMT level rather than EMR, while national competency profiles list it within EMR scope. Assisting a patient with their own prescribed nitroglycerin is more widely permitted than administering your own. Verify against your service protocol.

    Mechanism

    Nitroglycerin is metabolised in vascular smooth muscle to nitric oxide. Nitric oxide activates guanylyl cyclase, raising cyclic GMP, which reduces intracellular calcium and relaxes the muscle. Veins are far more sensitive to this than arteries, so at normal doses the dominant effect is venodilation. Blood pools in the periphery, less returns to the heart, preload falls, the ventricle is less stretched, and it does less work per beat. There is also some direct coronary dilation and relief of coronary spasm, but the preload effect is the main event.

    Why it works at that link and not another

    Ischemia is a supply and demand mismatch. You cannot easily increase supply through a blocked artery in the field, so you reduce demand instead. A less-filled ventricle has less wall tension, and wall tension is one of the largest determinants of myocardial oxygen consumption. This is also why it works so well in pulmonary edema: lowering preload directly lowers the pulmonary capillary pressure that is forcing fluid into the alveoli.

    What working looks like

    • Chest discomfort easing within a few minutes
    • In pulmonary edema, reduced work of breathing and improving saturation
    • A mild drop in blood pressure, which is expected
    • A throbbing headache, which is common and is evidence the dose was absorbed

    What it means if nothing happens

    Pain that does not respond to three doses is a concerning sign that suggests complete occlusion rather than reversible ischemia. Note also the old teaching that nitroglycerin relief confirms a cardiac cause is simply wrong: oesophageal spasm responds to it too, and plenty of genuine infarcts do not.

    Side effects are the drug doing its job elsewhere

    Nitroglycerin only does one thing, and every side effect is that one thing in a different vascular bed. Headache, often severe and throbbing, is cerebral vasodilation. Flushing is cutaneous vasodilation. Dizziness and hypotension are systemic venodilation taken slightly too far. Reflex tachycardia is the baroreceptors responding to the fall in pressure. And the interaction with erectile dysfunction drugs is not a quirk of chemistry: those drugs work on exactly the same pathway, and stacking them is simply the same mechanism twice.

    Cautions

    Systolic blood pressure below about 100

    Nitroglycerin lowers preload, and a patient who is already underfilled has no margin. In cardiogenic shock it can collapse the pressure entirely. Measure before every dose, not just the first.

    Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors

    Sildenafil, tadalafil, and vardenafil work on the same nitric oxide and cyclic GMP pathway from the other end, blocking the breakdown of cyclic GMP. Combined with a nitrate that increases its production, the result is profound, refractory hypotension. Avoid for 24 hours after sildenafil or vardenafil and 48 hours after tadalafil. You must ask, and patients frequently will not volunteer it.

    Right ventricular or inferior infarction

    A failing right ventricle depends on high filling pressure to produce any output. Dropping the preload can cause abrupt and severe hypotension. A patient whose pressure falls sharply after a first dose has probably told you their infarct is inferior.

    Severe aortic stenosis

    A fixed outflow obstruction means the ventricle needs high preload to push blood past it. Reducing preload can cause sudden collapse.

    Heart rate below 50 or above about 100

    Bradycardia limits the ability to compensate for the drop in preload; tachycardia already reduces filling time and worsens ischemia.

    Suspected head injury or raised intracranial pressure

    It dilates cerebral vessels, which raises intracranial blood volume and therefore pressure.
