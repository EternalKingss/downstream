    How this is put together

    Conditions are not filed under organs. They are filed under the substructure they actually damage, because that is what determines how they behave. Every condition attached to the pleural space produces the same mechanical failure — the lung cannot expand — and differs only in what filled the space and how fast.

    Once you have followed a few chains you will notice they converge. Falling oxygen looks the same whether it came from a blocked airway, a flooded alveolus, or a clot in a pulmonary artery. Those shared endpoints are written once, in the node library, and linked from every cascade that reaches them.

    Start from a sign instead

    The site runs forward by default: anatomy, then what breaks it, then what happens next. But in the field you start with a patient who is confused, or breathless, or grey. The reverse lookup runs the same chains backwards — pick what you can see, and every cascade that produces it is listed with the feature that separates it from the rest.
